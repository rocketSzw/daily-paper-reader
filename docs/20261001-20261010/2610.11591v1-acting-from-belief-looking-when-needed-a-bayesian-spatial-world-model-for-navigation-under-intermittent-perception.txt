Abstract — Robot navigation commonly uses wide-coverage, high-frequency sensing to reduce partial observability; this reliance becomes restrictive when another task temporarily redi-rects a shared sensor from navigation, interrupting navigation-relevant observations. We study navigation under intermittent perception: acting from an internal spatial belief and looking again only when execution needs a new observation, potentially freeing the shared sensor for other tasks between navigation observations. ALONE, a Bayesian spatial world model, prop-agates a structured spatial belief using executed actions and corrects it with selectively acquired observations; learned priors over common geometric structures infer unobserved structure from available observation history. It decodes the belief into a spatial estimate for the motion-planning module and predicts a reliability map expressing confidence in the estimate’s accuracy. ALONE requests an observation only if insufficient reliability hinders navigation and new evidence should make relevant-region spatial information more reliable; otherwise, it continues acting from the propagated belief. We instantiate ALONE for drone navigation with intermittent single-camera depth images. Across two simulated scene families, it achieves 98% and 97% closed-loop success at a 10 Hz decision rate. Among successful trials, median fractions of decision steps requiring a new depth observation are only 0.9% and 1.3%, respectively, demonstrating high navigation success with substantially reduced observation demand. Real-world indoor flight experiments further validate navigation under intermittent depth observations, with all 10 trials successful.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Most robot navigation systems treat perception as an always-on service. To reduce partial observability, they enlarge sensing coverage, observe at high frequency, fuse multiple sensors, and continually update a local map or state estimate [1]–[4]. This perception-first strategy is effective, but assumes that sensing can remain continuously directed toward navigation. The assumption becomes restrictive when another task redirects a shared sensor, interrupting navigation-relevant observations. Humans provide a familiar counterexample. A person can continue walking while visual attention briefly shifts to another task, relying on recent observations, self-motion, and priors to maintain an internal understanding of the surroundings. Such natural gaze behavior is task-dependent and is directed toward the information needed for ongoing action [5], [6]. How long this understanding remains useful depends on environmental predictability. A corridor supports predictable continuation and thus longer observation-free motion, whereas irregular forest geometry leaves upcoming obstacles weakly constrained. These priors support inference between observations; perception returns when reliability is insufficient for the next action. This observation motivates navigation under intermittent perception: can a robot act from an internal spatial belief and look again only when execution requires new evidence? To this end, the robot must infer current spatial structure arXiv:2610.11591v1 [cs.RO] 8 Oct 2026 from partial action–observation history, preserve alternatives not resolved by available evidence, and determine where its estimate is accurate enough for the motion-planning module’s current query. The observation policy should request new evidence only when insufficient reliability hinders navigation and an observation is expected to improve the relevant region. Existing methods provide individual pieces of this ca-pability. Mapping and spatial memory preserve acquired evidence [1]–[4], [7]–[12]; learned world models propagate latent states and predict hidden or future structure [13]– [19]; and belief-space planning or active perception adapts motion and sensing to uncertainty [20]–[23]. They do not by themselves provide one learned spatial belief that is propagated between observations, decoded into a spatial estimate and reliability map, and corrected by requested evidence. Prediction, action, and observation therefore remain separate rather than forming a closed loop. We call the resulting framework ALONE , reflecting its two operating modes: Acting from belief while perception is suspended and Looking Only when Needed for Execution. At its core, a decision-queryable Bayesian spatial world model maintains a structured spatial belief. Its action-conditioned prior performs learned spatial completion: learned geometric priors infer unobserved structure from the partial action– observation history. An observation-conditioned posterior corrects this completion when evidence returns. The belief yields a spatial estimate for the motion-planning module and a reliability map indicating confidence in its accuracy at each queried location. We term this probability query-space reliability. ALONE requests an observation only when insufficient reliability restricts navigation and the relevant region is expected to be visible; otherwise, it acts from the maintained belief. We instantiate ALONE for drone navigation using inter-mittent depth images from a single camera and a vector-quantized occupancy representation. ALONE preserves high navigation success while reducing observation demand by up to two orders of magnitude. Open-loop and ablation experiments further validate spatial-belief prediction and correction, learned spatial completion, query-space reliability, and the observation policy. 

Contributions. Our contributions are threefold: (1) aBayesian spatial world model that maintains a structured spatial belief through action-conditioned propagation and observation-conditioned correction, using learned geometric priors to infer unobserved structure and providing a spatial estimate together with an explicitly supervised reliability map for planning; (2) a navigation policy that uses spatial reliability to select motion actions and determine when new observations are needed, allowing the robot to continue acting from its belief between observations; and (3) an implementation for drone navigation using a single depth camera, evaluated through open-loop spatial inference, closed-loop navigation comparisons, and real-world flight experiments. The results demonstrate high navigation success with substantially re-duced observation demand. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Spatial Mapping and Memory 

Occupancy mapping integrates measurements into per-sistent free-space and occupancy estimates [1], [2], while probabilistic spatial models jointly estimate maps and agent states [3], [4], [7]. Neural, topological, volumetric, episodic, and semantic memories encode task-relevant spatial struc-ture [8]–[12]. These approaches primarily organize acquired evidence rather than propagate unresolved spatial hypotheses through observation-free motion or assess whether the result-ing local structure supports a motion-planning query. ALONE instead propagates a learned categorical spatial belief with executed actions and corrects it with later observations. 

B. Predictive World Models 

Latent world models learn action-conditioned dynamics for planning and behavior learning [14], [15]. Recent work extends prediction to navigation, visual representations, per-sistent 3D scenes, and occupancy forecasting [16]–[19]. Yet prediction alone does not establish whether a spatial estimate reliably supports current action selection. ALONE uses learned geometric priors to infer unobserved structure and predicts a reliability map for the estimate, so the same belief supports both action selection and observation decisions. 

C. Uncertainty-Aware Planning and Active Perception 

Belief-space planning incorporates state uncertainty into trajectory optimization [20], [22], while active perception selects sensing actions or viewpoints using information gain or task-relevant uncertainty [21], [23]. These methods commonly assume that a suitable belief or uncertainty estimate is available. ALONE instead learns, propagates, queries, and corrects the spatial belief underlying its observation decision. It requests an observation when insufficient reliability restricts navigation and the responsible region is expected to be visible; the returned evidence corrects the maintained belief, closing the loop among prediction, action, observation, and correction. ALONE has two coupled modes. Without a navigation observation, it propagates the belief with the executed action; the motion-planning module generates candidates from the decoded estimate, and the navigation policy selects one to execute. Observation intervals thus adapt to environ-mental predictability and decision-relevant uncertainty. We instantiate ALONE for collision-aware drone navigation using intermittent depth images from a single camera. At each step, the motion-planning module generates candidate actions from the decoded spatial estimate, and ALONE’s navigation policy uses reliability to select the executed action and decide whether to request depth. Executed motion propagates the belief, while requested depth corrects it. 

A. Occupancy Representation and Supervision 

The navigation state and motion-planning query are bi-nary occupancy fields in an ego-centric, yaw-aligned voxel frame with a gravity-aligned vertical axis: X = Y =

{0, 1}nx×ny ×nz and ψ = Id . Actions are relative 3-D poses and runtime observations are depth images. Simulation supplies dense aligned occupancy as Osup ∈ Y , so Sup Y =Id ; zero–one discrepancy with a zero error threshold makes adequacy equivalent to voxelwise occupancy agreement. With real data, Sup Y may instead construct a query-space target from lidar point clouds or another supervisory sensor. Following OccWorld [19], a standard vector-quantized autoencoder maps a dense occupancy field to an h × w code map with codebook size K and decodes it back to occupancy. Each latent location summarizes a multivoxel receptive field, allowing one categorical marginal to represent alternative local shapes. Accordingly, Struct = Sup Z = Enc vq and 

Read = Dec vq . The maintained belief is a K × h × w

tensor whose componentwise MAP code map is decoded as the spatial estimate byt. The adequacy decoder provides its reliability map πt = Pω (bt).

E. Real-World Drone Navigation 

We evaluate ALONE with intermittent depth observations on a drone equipped with Intel RealSense D435i and T265 sensors in an indoor Lattice environment (Fig. 1). D435i depth corrects the belief, while T265 visual–inertial relative-motion estimates propagate it, matching the simulation inputs. For real-world flight, we use a more conservative configuration than in simulation, requesting observations sooner to limit belief extrapolation and prevent boundary-wall collisions in the confined space. Each trial starts at a random position and requires 10 m of forward navigation. ALONE completes all 10 trials while acquiring depth at only 7.0% of decision steps, showing that the maintained belief supports action selection and flight between observations with substantially reduced observation demand. VI. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We presented ALONE, a Bayesian spatial world model that uses learned geometric priors to maintain spatial predictions between observations and reliability to guide when perception resumes. In two simulated scene families, ALONE achieves 98% and 97% navigation success at a 10 Hz decision rate, with median observation rates of 0.9% and 1.3% of decision steps in successful trials, respectively. Real-world flight exper-iments further demonstrate navigation from the maintained belief between depth observations, with all 10 trials completed successfully. These results support substantially reduced observation demand in the tested environments, potentially freeing the shared sensor for other tasks. Future work will investigate explicit sensor allocation between navigation and other tasks, extend ALONE to dynamic environments, and incorporate semantic knowledge to infer complete object structure from partial observations.
