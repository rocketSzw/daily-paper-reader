# Uncertainty-Aware Vision-Based Autonomous Aerial Refueling 

Minhyuk Jang 1,∗, Thinh Nguyen 1,∗, Vivek Khatana 1,2, Sandeep Banik 1, Naira Hovakimyan 1, Petros G. Voulgaris 2

Abstract — Probe-and-drogue refueling (PDR) requires accu-rate terminal alignment of a receiver probe with an unin-strumented, dynamically moving drogue despite uncertainty in both relative-state estimation and closed-loop motion. Ex-isting vision-based docking approaches provide relative-state estimates, but generally do not quantify estimation and opera-tional uncertainty in a way that informs the control authority required for robust docking performance. We present an uncertainty-aware vision-based PDR docking architecture that fuses keypoint-based drogue measurements, differential GPS, and avionics in an extended Kalman filter. Split conformal calibration characterizes near-contact error in the fused probe-to-drogue displacement estimate, while a covariance-based op-erational uncertainty quantification designs position-dispersion limits along with corrective-command variance budgets under modeled handoff variability and disturbances. The resulting uncertainty statistics define geometric and command margins for a range-conditioned deterministic predictive controller. In high-fidelity FlightGear/JSBSim simulations with four con-troller variants and 50 runs per variant, the combined-margin controller achieved 45/50 centered basket-plane crossings versus 34/50 for nominal MPC. Median planar crossing error de-creased from 0.231 to 0.175 m and the empirical 90th percentile from 0.403 to 0.286 m, while the roll-command RMS increased from 0.22 ◦ to 0.48 ◦.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Autonomous aerial refueling (AAR) extends aircraft en-durance without landing. One approach is probe-and-drogue refueling (PDR), in which the receiver guides its probe into a drogue towed by the tanker. This docking maneuver is particularly challenging because the flexible hose–drogue assembly responds to atmospheric disturbances, tanker wake, and receiver bow-wave effects, while receiver corrections are constrained by aircraft response and command authority [1], [2]. Small errors near contact can cause the probe to miss the drogue despite a well-tracked nominal trajectory [3]. Vision provides close-range measurements without in-strumenting the drogue [4], [5]. The docking control sys-tem must translate uncertain relative-position estimates into control inputs that achieve alignment despite disturbances and variable approach conditions. We distinguish estimation uncertainty , the error in the fused probe-to-drogue vision-aided multisensor estimate, from operational uncertainty , the trajectory dispersion induced by variable handoff conditions, disturbances affecting drogue motion, and receiver-response error. A nominal trajectory through the basket center alone does not indicate how much uncertainty can be accommo-dated or what corrective response is required. We therefore seek a systematic basis for selecting planning margins that reflects both uncertainty and receiver dynamics. To this end, we combine data-driven uncertainty calibration with a model-based feasibility assessment. Conformal prediction converts estimator error into a geometric allowance through calibrated prediction sets, with coverage specified under the calibration assumptions [6]–[8]. Covariance uncertainty quantification [9]–[11] evaluates whether a desired near-contact dispersion can be supported within control-command budgets under operational uncertainty. Together with the estimator allowance, the resulting position and command uncertainty statistics configure a range-conditioned online model predictive controller (MPC) that generates roll, pitch, and airspeed references for the existing automatic flight control system (AFCS). The major contributions of this work are as follows: 

(i) a two-source uncertainty decomposition for vision-based PDR for AAR, in which conformal calibration maps fused-estimator error to a geometric allowance and offline co-variance design maps operational uncertainty to coupled po-sition/command margins; (ii) a computationally light offline– online controller architecture in which these margins config-ure a deterministic range-conditioned MPC above an existing AFCS; and (iii) a high-fidelity simulation ablation and feasi-bility study that reveals the tradeoff between retained capture tolerance and control-command variability, while isolating the effects of estimator and operational-uncertainty margins on crossing accuracy and command effort. II. R ELATED WORK 

Relative sensing and estimation. Refueling navigation must distinguish aircraft-relative motion from motion of the drogue itself. Vision/GPS fusion estimates tanker–receiver relative position [12], while stereo/inertial fusion supports relative navigation without GPS [13]. Terminal docking additionally requires direct drogue observations. Monocular tracking and structural geometry provide drogue position and pose measurements [4], [5], while learned keypoints support geometric pose recovery [14] and neural perception has been integrated with unscented filtering [15]. Recent work has further improved pose estimation in challenging vi-sual conditions using lightweight learned architectures [14]. These methods primarily improve the quality and robustness of the visual measurement itself. In contrast, we focus on the residual uncertainty remaining after vision is fused with navigation and avionics, and on converting that fused-estimator error into a geometric allowance for docking. 

Docking control under uncertainty. Vision-based tra-jectory tracking follows drogue motion using relative mea-surements [16]. For uncertain aircraft response, L1 adap-tive flight control provides a priori performance bounds and a time-delay margin bounded under an assumption of known drogue positions; its AAR evaluation includes wake-induced aerodynamic uncertainty and partial loss of control effectiveness [17]. More recent work has derived closed-loop docking-performance bounds under uncertain drogue motion [18] and developed robust PDR docking controllers under nonlinearities, disturbances, and actuator uncertainty [19], [20]. Terminal iterative learning uses earlier docking errors to update initial positions and feedforward commands [2], while data-driven safety assessment predicts docking outcomes to guide the approach strategy [21]. Our approach is complementary: we connect uncertain docking conditions to the receiver’s corrective capability and seek to determine how much geometric tolerance and corrective-command authority should be reserved before online plan-ning to accommodate quantified uncertainty. 

Uncertainty quantification and planning. Conformal prediction provides finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability [6], [7], with extensions that quantify cover-age loss when this assumption is violated [8]. In robotics, calibrated keypoint sets propagate image uncertainty into pose regions [22], while calibrated state-estimation regions support measurement-robust barrier-function control under stochastic sensor noise [23]. For dynamic environments, trajectory-prediction regions also support MPC among mov-ing agents [24], [25]. Beyond estimation error, disturbances induce state uncertainty whose evolution depends on the system dynamics. Covariance shaping addresses this depen-dence by propagating dynamic uncertainty through stochastic models and enforcing state and input constraints under un-certainty [10]. Risk-aware formulations similarly couple state and input statistics through stochastic dynamics [9], [11], including applications to motion planning with collision-avoidance constraints [26]. Existing approaches therefore provide strong tools for calibrated perception uncertainty, stochastic state propagation, and uncertainty-aware con-trol, but these mechanisms are generally treated sepa-rately. In PDR docking, estimation error and dynamics-dependent operational uncertainty consume the finite cap-ture and command budgets in different ways. We there-fore use conformal calibration to determine the geometric allowance associated with fused-estimator error, and an of-fline covariance-feasibility design to determine compatible position-dispersion and corrective-command margins under operational uncertainty. Only these margins enter the online MPC, which remains deterministic. III. S YSTEM MODEL AND ESTIMATION

B. Uncertainty Characterization 

Conformal Prediction Results. We collect calibration data from 7 independent PDR runs using a baseline LQR con-troller [38] and retain samples with depth ≤ 1 m to reflect the terminal estimator margin (20). 10% uniformly random subsampling is performed on each run. Exchangeability condition is not met: calibration data points from the same run are not independent, and the deployed controller and flight conditions differ. However, our procedure keeps the total variation term in (12) small in two ways. First, we retain the distribution of online PDR operations as opposed to grid sampling [22] that could cause distribution shift. Second, random subsampling spaces the samples apart in time and mixes flight conditions, weakening the temporal correlation that causes dependence and nonexchangeability. We use a calibration dataset with nc = 481 and target error rate αc = 0 .1. On one held-out closed-loop flight, 716 /790

Crossing Accuracy and Command Activity. With tracking costs fixed, MPC with both margins achieved the highest centered-crossing count and lowest median and P90 error among the four variants (Table II and Fig. 4). Covariance-derived margins account for most of the improvement in median crossing error, reducing it from 0.231 m to 0.176 m. Adding the conformal estimator allowance changes the me-dian only marginally, but further reduces the empirical P90 from 0.304 m to 0.286 m and increases centered crossings from 43 /50 to 45 /50 . This suggests that the operational-uncertainty margins primarily improve nominal centering, while the estimator allowance provides additional robustness in the upper tail. The combined controller also exhibits substantially smaller empirical lateral bias: the mean lateral crossing error shifted from −0.180 m for nominal MPC to 0.014 m with both margins. This improvement came with greater roll-command RMS, increasing from 0.22 ◦ for nominal MPC to 0.48 ◦ with both margins. Figure 5(a) shows trajectories with both margins on the offline covariance envelope, showing convergence toward the basket from varied handoff offsets. Figure 5(b) shows similar roll activity near handoff but sustained higher activity with both margins near contact, indicating that the additional effort is concentrated in final alignment.

VI. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We presented a vision-based docking architecture that translates estimation and operational uncertainty into geo-metric and control input margins. Conformal calibration pro-vides the estimator allowance, while covariance uncertainty quantification provides the position dispersion along with the control input margins required to counter the operational uncertainty. This provides a principled basis for incorporating uncertainty bounds and corresponding control input margins while retaining a deterministic MPC. The high-fidelity ex-periments demonstrate that the proposed controller achieved 45/50 centered crossings versus 34/50 for nominal MPC. Future work will extend conformal calibration to trajectory-wide uncertainty tubes for planning within a corri-dor of autonomy [3]. and integrate L1 adaptive control [39] for improved transient response and disturbance rejection.
