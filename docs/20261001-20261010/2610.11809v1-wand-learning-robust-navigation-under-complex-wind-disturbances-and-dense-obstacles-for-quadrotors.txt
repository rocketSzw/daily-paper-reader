# WAND: Learning Robust Navigation under Complex Wind Disturbances and Dense Obstacles for Quadrotors 

Zhonghan Tang 1,2, Chenhui Li 2, Shuai Liang 2, Zhongrui You 2, Jianan Li 2, Bin Zhao 2,3, Zhigang Wang 2, and Xuelong Li 4

Abstract —Robust navigation in cluttered environments re-mains a fundamental challenge for quadrotors, particularly when strong wind disturbances arise, which perturb vehicle dynamics, limit control authority, and substantially increase collision risk. Existing learning-based navigation policies typically rely on obstacle perception and proprioceptive observations, requiring the policy to infer time-varying disturbance effects implicitly and thereby limiting robustness under partial observability. This pa-per proposes WAND (Wind-Aware Navigation with Disturbance Estimation), a reinforcement learning framework for navigation under time-varying wind disturbances in dense obstacle fields. Specifically, WAND estimates wind-induced disturbance acceler-ation from historical proprioceptive states using a Temporal Con-volutional Network (TCN). This estimation is integrated into the policy via a zero-initialized residual module, WindAdapter , while simultaneously providing feedforward compensation for low-level control. The dual use of the estimate couples disturbance-conditioned navigation with feedforward disturbance rejection. Across 12 wind-disturbed simulation settings, WAND improved the observed success rate by 8.3 percentage points on average relative to feedforward compensation alone. Controlled opposite-crosswind experiments further showed wind-direction-dependent trajectory adaptation. In indoor fan-induced flight tests, WAND succeeded in 18 of 20 trials, demonstrating the feasibility of real-time onboard navigation. 

Index Terms —Aerial Systems: Perception and Autonomy, Re-inforcement Learning, Autonomous Vehicle Navigation

Traditional navigation stacks separate geometric planning from low-level control. Geometry-oriented planners [8]–[10] focus on trajectory feasibility, whereas robust planners [11], [12] and predictive controllers [13] account for disturbances or tracking uncertainty but depend on assumed models and sufficient control authority. Strong, rapidly varying wind can therefore leave residual tracking errors that erode trajectory clearance in confined environments. In contrast, learning-based methods integrate perception and control through learned navigation policies. NavRL [14] learns safe navigation in dynamic clutter, while end-to-end ap-proaches map LiDAR [15], [16] or visual features [17] to low-level commands and support sim-to-real transfer [18]. This partial observability complicates robust wind-aware navigation. To address this issue, we propose WAND, a wind-aware RL framework for safe navigation in windy, cluttered environ-ments. A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) estimates wind-induced disturbance acceleration from proprioceptive histories, and the proposed WindAdapter encodes this estimate into wind-conditioned features. A. Hierarchical Navigation in Cluttered Environments 

Autonomous navigation in cluttered environments has been widely studied within hierarchical planning frameworks. Rep-resentative methods such as Fast-Planner [8] and EGO-Planner [9] achieve real-time collision-free trajectory generation by ex-ploiting geometric constraints derived from occupancy maps. More recent works [10], [19] further improve computational efficiency and trajectory quality in dense obstacle fields, demonstrating strong performance in structured navigation tasks. However, these planners are primarily designed for geometric feasibility and typically rely on the low-level con-troller to track the reference trajectory with sufficient accuracy. To improve tracking robustness against external disturbances, prior works have explored disturbance-compensation strategies [13]. Nevertheless, in most hierarchical pipelines, disturbance rejection is primarily handled by the tracking controller. As a result, the planned trajectory may not explicitly account for time-varying aerodynamic effects, and severe wind gusts can drive the quadrotor beyond the intended safety margins, increasing the risk of collision. 

B. Learning-Based Navigation 

Learning-based navigation directly learns obstacle-avoidance and goal-reaching behaviors from data. Early learning-based approaches demonstrated sim-to-real navigation in clutter with CAD2RL [20] and privileged learning for high-speed flight in unstructured environments [21]. Building on this, Song et al. [17] combine imitation and reinforcement learning through a privileged learning-by-cheating framework that tightly couples perception and control. Xu et al. [15] and Fan et al. [16] design task-relevant LiDAR representations for safe and reactive navigation, while NavRL [14] addresses dynamic environments with moving obstacles through tailored state representations and adaptive architectures. Yu et al. [22] leverage memory-augmented latent representations with varying-speed strategies to enhance navigation performance. History-conditioned latent adaptation has been applied to quadrotor control by inferring a latent representation of vehicle and environment parameters from recent state–action histories and conditioning the controller on that representation [23]. In WAND, temporal conditioning is grounded in a supervised estimate of wind-induced disturbance acceleration, thereby making the conditioning signal physically interpretable. Com-plementary onboard estimators recover wind or external forces from proprioceptive or visual–inertial measurements [24]– [26], while adaptive learning and control methods improve dynamics prediction or trajectory tracking under wind dis-turbances [27], [28]. WAND integrates these directions by using the explicit disturbance estimate both to adapt obstacle-aware navigation actions and to provide low-level feedforward compensation. III. C. Real-World Experiments 

To evaluate onboard deployment, we conduct real-world navigation experiments in cluttered environments under fan-induced aerodynamic disturbances, as shown in Fig. 7. The quadrotor carries an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX and a Livox Mid-360 3D LiDAR. The LiDAR stream is processed by Super-LIO [34] for real-time state estimation and is also converted into obstacle observations for the policy. The TCN estimator and PPO navigation module contain 196,547 and 146,151 parameters, respectively, for 342,698 parameters in total. Both modules run onboard at 30 Hz, with an average combined inference time of 11 .76 ms . The desired acceleration commands are sent to the PX4 flight controller through its control interface. V. CONCLUSIONS This paper presented WAND, a wind-aware reinforcement learning framework for quadrotor navigation in cluttered en-vironments under time-varying aerodynamic disturbances. By combining a navigation policy with a wind disturbance estima-tor and the proposed WindAdapter module, WAND enables the quadrotor to adapt its motion strategy according to the wind condition.
