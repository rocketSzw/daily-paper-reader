# Constructive Safety-Critical Control for a Class of Underactuated Systems: A Hierarchical Approach 

Massimiliano de Sa and Aaron D. Ames 

Abstract — Underactuated systems with nontrivial geometry are abundant in practical robotic problems. However, little is known about constructive safety-critical control design in the presence of underactuation. In this work, we provide a constructive framework for synthesizing safe control architec-tures and control barrier functions for a class of quadrotor-like underactuated systems. By studying the geometry of their un-deractuation, we characterize when these systems have natural layered architectures useful for safety-critical control design. Using these architectures, we devise a constructive, hierarchical CBF synthesis procedure and provide conditions under which it succeeds. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Underactuated mechanical systems—systems with fewer degrees of actuation than degrees of freedom—comprise an important class of control systems ubiquitous in mod-ern robotic problems. Due to their underspecified nature, underactuated systems are substantially more challenging to control than their fully-actuated counterparts. Further-more, numerous important underactuated control systems— for instance, the quadrotor—have manifold-valued states, compelling controller designs to contend with both under-actuation and nontrivial state space geometry. In practice, such systems are subject to a host of safety constraints; quadrotors, for instance, must avoid collisions with their environment. Control barrier functions (CBFs) 

have been established as a leading framework for the specifi-cation of safety constraints and design of safety-critical con-trollers [1], [2]. To leverage this framework, and the host of safety-critical control design techniques it enables, one must first construct a control barrier function. Though the process of synthesizing CBFs for fully-actuated systems is relatively well understood, less is known about the underactuated case. Recently, a hierarchical perspective on synthesizing CBFs for complex, potentially underactuated systems has emerged [3]–[7]. Within this paradigm, one first designs a CBF for a reduced-order model, a virtual model for which CBF design is simple. Then, one assumes there exists a controller for the full-order system that tracks the inputs to the reduced-order model, as well as a Lyapunov certificate of tracking. Under various assumptions (e.g. sufficiently fast tracking, bounded full-to-reduced-order model error), the Lyapunov certificate can be combined with a CBF for the reduced-order model to produce a CBF for the full system.

Although these hierarchical techniques have demonstrated considerable success as a framework, little is known about hierarchies from the constructive perspective. Indeed, given a complex, underactuated system, it is unknown how to choose a reduced-order model satisfying the requisite tracking and boundedness conditions (nor whether one exists), nor how to find the tracking Lyapunov certificate required for CBF construction (nor whether one exists). Further, the nontrivial role of geometry—which poses fundamental obstructions to tracking control [8], [9]—has yet to be examined. To better understand when these unknowns have concrete solutions, we perform a geometric study of layered control architectures. We are driven by two principal directions. On one front, we aim to determine how and when a layered architecture can be designed. Here, we ask what conditions enable the existence of “natural” layered architectures, and how inputs to reduced-order models can be transformed into references for the full-order system to track. On another front, given an architecture in which a safe controller for a reduced-order model is tracked by a controller for the full system, we ask how and when safety certificates for the full system can be assembled in closed form. In pursuing these two directions, we focus on a class of quadrotor-like geometric mechanical control systems, for which concrete answers are available and interpretable. By leveraging symmetries of these systems, and drawing analogies to existing quadrotor control architectures [10], we characterize when natural choices of architectures exist for these systems and when they can be certified by constructive, closed-form control barrier functions. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 1) Develop and characterize shape maps as a means of dealing with underactuation and transforming virtual arXiv:2610.10987v1 [eess.SY] 7 Oct 2026 control inputs into references for underactuated systems. 2) Use symmetries and shape maps to design layered con-trol architectures for a class of underactuated systems. 3) Apply these layered architectures to devise a construc-tive, hierarchical CBF synthesis procedure for a class of underactuated systems. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we review the necessary preliminaries. In Section III, we discuss architectures, shape maps, and their existence. In Section IV, we discuss CBF synthesis, and in Section V, we provide concluding remarks. II. P RELIMINARIES

In the literature, control barrier functions for complex, po-tentially underactuated systems are often constructed hierar-chically from reduced-order models. For mechanical systems, for instance, hierarchical CBF synthesis typically proceeds as follows. Given a SMCS on a configuration manifold Q,a set of safe configurations C0 ⊂ G is specified, where G is a smaller manifold related to Q by a projection π : Q → G.One designs a CBF for Σ with safe set C such that vq ∈ C 

implies q is safe with respect to C0, satisfying π(q) ∈ C 0.The design procedure is as follows. On G, choose a kinematic reduced-order model, a vector field κ that is safe with respect to C0. Assuming that the full mechanical system can track κ in a certifiable manner, the certificate of tracking can be combined with a certificate of safety for κ to produce the desired CBF for the full system [3], [5], [14]. Though this hierarchical process—determining a safe vec-tor field, tracking it, and finding certificates of tracking and safety—is well understood for fully-actuated systems, comparatively little is known about the underactuated case. This leads to the following motivating problem of this work. 

Problem 1. Let Σ be an underactuated SMCS on Q and 

π : Q → G be a projection to a manifold G. Given a safe configuration set C0 ⊂ G, and a safe vector field κ on G,1) How and when can a κ-tracking controller be designed and certified by a closed-form tracking certificate? 2) When can the tracking certificate be used to synthesize a CBF for the safe configuration set ˜C0 = π−1(C0)?To gain insight into this problem, we begin by examining one of the most important underactuated control systems: the quadrotor on SE (3) . C0. To track κ, a popular strategy is to use yet another hierarchical control design, as visualized in Fig. 2 [10]. A. Layered Architecture

Although h is a CBF like any other, and therefore can be implemented directly in a CBF-QP safety filter, it is often more practical to leverage a hierarchical perspective in the use of h. Instead of using h as an end-to-end CBF, one may first design a safe velocity controller κ and track it using the proposed hierarchical control design. Theorem 2 provides conditions under which h is a certificate of safety for the full architecture, even if it is not used in an end-to-end safety filter. This use of h is reflective of the common design practice in which a safe controller is designed for a simple system and tracked by a performant low-level controller. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we performed a constructive, hierarchical study of safety-critical control for underactuated systems. We introduced shape maps, providing precise conditions for the existence of a correspondence between input directions in G

and shapes in S. Using these maps, we developed a layered control architecture enabling a hierarchical CBF synthesis procedure.
