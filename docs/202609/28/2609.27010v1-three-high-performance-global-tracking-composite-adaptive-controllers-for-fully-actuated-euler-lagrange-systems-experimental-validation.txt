## Abstract 

## Three adaptive global tracking controllers for fully actuated Euler–Lagrange systems, with verifiable 

## performance improvement over existing designs, are reported in this letter. Two of these controllers ensure global exponential convergence under a weak interval excitation condition. Besides, one of the proposed controllers features a simple adaptive PID-like structure that—unlike classical solutions—avoids the need for additional filtering. We adopt a composite adaptation architecture, invoke a novel parameterization of the system dynamics and use a high performance estimation scheme recently introduced in the literature. Real-time experiments and a comparative study with a learning-based adaptive controller on a two–degrees-of-freedom manipulator arm illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controllers. 

# 1 Introduction 

## The problem of designing adaptive global tracking controllers for fully actuated Euler-Lagrange (EL) systems has been investigated for a long time, with numerous solutions being reported, for example in: [14, 19–21]. To prove the good performance of these controllers— e.g. , parameter convergence or exponen-tial stability—it is necessary to assume strong excita-tion conditions, for instance, persistency of excitation 

## (PE). It is well-known that PE is a very strong re-quirement that, for instance, is not satisfied in regula-tion tasks. An experimental evaluation of several pa-rameter estimation methods for a robot manipulator was presented in [31], where the advantages and dis-advantages of the classical methods are highlighted, with excitation being the major obstacle. After [19], a large number of adaptive trajectory tracking con-trollers have been published up to this day. For ex-ample [5–8]. Notable advances in addressing this lim-itation have been achieved using learning-based and neural networks techniques [1–3, 32]; however, the computational cost increases significantly, rendering high-computation-demand adaptive controllers diffi-cult to implement. A survey on composite adaptation and learn-ing techniques to improve parameter convergence in adaptive control for robot control applications is pre-sented in [4]. Unfortunately, the majority of works mentioned previously have a complicated parameter-ization of the linear regression equation (LRE), and parameter convergence is ensured by imposing the 1arXiv:2609.27010v1 [eess.SY] 22 Sep 2026 unfeasible persistent excitation (PE) condition. In this paper we propose some new controllers that over-come this limitation. Although high-quality tracking is indeed the pri-mary objective in robotics, estimating model param-eters remains valuable because the parameters encode the system’s physical characteristics. As discussed in [38], parameter estimation from input–output data is an important step in the analysis of dynamic sys-tems, since the parameters often describe the stability and control behaviour of the system and help estab-lish the adequacy of the mathematical model. Know-ing the system parameters can also be important to compute manipulability indices [39, 40] and posture indices [41], since these performance measures typi-cally require full knowledge of the system parameters. We present in the paper three new controllers with verifiable performance improvement assuming only the weak interval excitation (IE) condition [13] that, as is well-known is strictly weaker than PE and sat-isfied for all standard control tasks—including regu-lation. Under the IE assumption, we establish global 

## exponential convergence of the position and velocity tracking errors, as well as of the estimated uncertain parameters, for two of these controllers; whereas for the third one, we prove that the velocity and parame-ter estimation errors converge asymptotically to zero, while the tracking error remains bounded. The following key modifications are instrumental for the success of our designs. 

## M1 The use of a composite adaptation architecture 

## [10, 16, 20]—that is known to ensure robustness properties which are stronger than the one of the classical direct or indirect schemes 

## M2 We invoke a novel parameterization of the sys-tem dynamics [17], which has shown to be more effective than the classical one [12]. 

## M3 We use a the high performance least squares plus dynamic regression extension and mix-ing (LS+DREM) estimation scheme recently re-ported in [15], which ensures exponential con-vergence of the estimates, imposing only the ex-tremely weak IE assumption. Moreover, it has shown to be robust in the presence of noise—an essential feature in the application at hand. In comparison with existing literature the main con-tributions are: 

## C1 Compared with our previous result [37], this note presents an additional adaptive PID-like con-troller based on measured tracking errors, which ensures exponential convergence. Regarding one of the previous solutions reported new stability conclusions are derivated using Matrosov’s the-orem. 

## C2 We propose three composite adaptive trajectory-tracking controllers that achieve the tracking control objective and guarantee convergence of the parameter estimates to their true values without imposing the persistent excitation con-dition. 

## C3 Compared with composite adaptive controllers that do not impose persistent excitation, and in particular with learning-based methods such as [28], the proposed schemes have verifiably lower computational cost. 

## C4 Compared with the classical PD +adaptive con-troller of [19], two of the proposed controllers incorporate a novel parameterization that en-hances robustness in the presence of measure-ment noise. The remainder of the paper is organized as fol-lows. Section 2 presents the system dynamics and the problem formulation. In Section 3 we give some preliminary material used in the sequel. The main re-sults of the paper, namely three new adaptive global tracking controllers, are presented in Section 4. The experimental implementation is detailed in Section 5. Finally, the conclusions are summarized in Section 6.

# 2.2 Problem formulation 

## The control problem is to design global tracking adaptive controllers for the EL system (3) whose transient behavior and robustness outperforms the existing solutions to the problem, in particular the classical adaptive controller [19] and its robust ver-sion [9]. To achieve this goal we appeal to the following recent results. 

## R1. We derive the linear regression equation (LRE) instrumental to estimate the system parameters with the new parametrization of EL systems ob-tained using the power-balance equation [17]. It has been shown that—due to its lower dimension and considerably simpler analytic expressions—this parameterization is more effective than the standard parameterization first reported by [12] and used in all adaptive controllers [9, 19]. 

## R2. Use the first stage of the LS+DREM pro-cedure recently reported in [15], to generate, from the previous power-balance LRE, a new set of scalar 

## LRE. As shown in [15], the new scalar regressor has the distinguishing feature of satisfying the key persistent excitation (PE) condition imposing only the weakest assumption of identifiability of the power-balance LRE [11]. As is well-known, PE of the regressor ensures global exponential convergence of the parameter estimates that, in its turn, guarantees strong robustness properties for the estimator [18]. Equipped with these two elements we propose three new composite adaptive controllers, which achieve the desired global tracking objective. Com-posite adaptive controllers, that combine direct and indirect components, have been reported for linear systems [10], for robotic applications [20, 27] and for a class of nonlinear systems [16]. In all these cases, been effectively exploited to enhance the robustness 

## of the adaptive controllers.

# 4 Three New Composite Adap-tive Controllers 

## In this section we present the main results of the pa-per, which consists of the design of three new com-posite adaptive trajectory tracking controllers for the EL system (3)—all of them relying on the use of the scalar LREs (17). The first scheme is a modified Slotine-Li controller, while the second one is a to-tally new PID-like adaptive tracking controller that has the interesting feature of avoiding the filtering operations [19] but operates, instead, on the actual tracking error signals. Additionally, the third one contains a modification of the second one, so that exponential convergence can be ensured. For one controller we prove global convergence of both, the parameter and the tracking errors under the weakest IE assumption. Moreover, for two controllers we are able to prove that the convergence is exponential .Throughout the paper we make the standard as-sumption that the desired trajectory q⋆(t) ∈ Rnq ,as well as its first and second order derivatives, are bounded.

# 4.4 Tuning guidelines for controller gains 

## The proposed controllers of Proposition 3-5 include: a proportional term KP ˜q acting on the joint posi-tion tracking error, a derivative term KD ˙˜q acting on the joint velocity error, and an integral/adaptive term shaped by the scalar gain kI multiplying the estimated-parameter contribution. Therefore, clas-sical PID-tuning intuition can be used as a start-ing point: KP primarily stiffens the position error dynamics, KD increases damping and reduces over-shoot, and kI governs the aggressiveness of the adap-tive/integral action (faster parameter adaptation ver-sus noise sensitivity) see for instance [42]. Regarding the LS +DREM estimator, the estimator gains can be selected following standard guidelines for least-squares schemes with forgetting factor, as discussed in adaptive control textbooks (see, e.g., [18]).

# 5.1 A comparative study 

## In this section, we present two controllers from the literature that are used to perform a comparative study against our proposals, namely, the compos-ite learning-based trajectory-tracking controller of [27] and the classical direct adaptive PD +adaptive-compensation controller of [19]. As stated in the in-troduction, the adaptive trajectory-tracking compos-ite learning-based controller of [27] ensures exponen-tial convergence to zero of the joint position error, the velocity error, and the estimation error, that is, the difference between the estimated unknown parame-ters and their true values, without imposing the PE condition. Instead, it relies on the relaxed IE condi-tion. Therefore, it ensures essentially the same stabil-ity conclusions as the controllers of Propositions 3–5, 14 making it an ideal candidate for comparison with an up-to-date controller from the literature. On the other hand, the classical direct adaptive trajectory-tracking controller of [19] ensures conver-gence to zero of the joint position and velocity errors. However, convergence to zero of the parametric error is guaranteed only if the system dynamics regressor satisfies the PE condition. Thus, to illustrate the im-portance of the excitation condition, we experimen-tally evaluate the controller of [19] and compare its results with those obtained using the controllers of Propositions 3–5. Particular emphasis is placed on the comparison with the controller of Proposition 3, since both controllers have almost the same structure. The results show that the proposed improvement not only enhances the tracking performance, but also im-proves the convergence of the parameter estimates.

# 6 Conclusions 

## Three adaptive global trajectory-tracking controllers for fully actuated Euler–Lagrange systems have been introduced in this paper. In all three cases, the joint position tracking objective is achieved. For the first and third proposals, it has been shown that the joint position and velocity tracking errors, as well as the parameter estimation error, converge to zero expo-nentially under a weak interval excitation condition, using the power-balance parameterization and the LS +DREM estimator. For the second proposal, it has been shown that the joint position and veloc-ity tracking errors and the parameter estimation er-ror converge asymptotically to zero under an interval excitation condition when using the energy-based re-22 gressor. Experimental validation on a two-degree-of-freedom robotic arm was conducted to evaluate the performance of the three controllers, and the results were compared with those of a composite learning-based adaptive controller (CLAC) that guarantees similar stability properties under comparable excita-tion conditions. The experimental tests showed that the proposed estimation algorithm is able to estimate the unknown constant parameters of the mechani-cal system and achieves the trajectory-tracking con-trol objective, as predicted by the stability analysis. Finally, the performance-index results indicate that, compared to the CLAC and the classical PD +AC controller, the proposed controllers achieve improved performance in selected indices and a significantly lower computational cost.
