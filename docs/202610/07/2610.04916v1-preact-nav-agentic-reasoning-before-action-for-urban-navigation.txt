ABSTRACT 

Urban navigation requires embodied agents to pursue long-horizon goals through local decisions based on egocentric observations. However, existing agentic naviga-tion methods often struggle to translate distant goals into coherent local decisions in large-scale physical environments. Their reliance on linguistic reasoning over transient observations or limited history constrains anticipation of the consequences of actions and future conditions, despite the importance of such foresight for navi-gating long and complex urban routes. To bridge this gap, we propose PreAct-Nav, an agentic navigation framework that equips frozen policies with anticipatory rea-soning for robust urban navigation. Our central idea is to anchor local decisions in persistent medium-horizon subgoals, assess the consequences of predicted ac-tions before execution, and continually update the reasoning context using actual outcomes. At its core, a navigation memory module maintains the active subgoal and relevant experience across decisions, translating distant goals into actionable intermediate objectives. We further introduce a predictive world sandbox that uses an action-conditioned world model (AC-WM) to forecast world dynamics conditioned on candidate movements. A vision-language model (VLM) reasoner interprets these predictions under the current subgoal to retain or revise actions. After execution, real observations are used to assess outcomes, correct inconsistent assumptions, and update memory to continue or reformulate the subgoal. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that the proposed PreAct-Nav improves action selection through memory updates and visual prediction, with more pronounced gains on longer routes and routes with more turns. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Urban navigation requires embodied agents to reach a destination described in language through a sequence of local movements (Mei et al., 2026b). The agent sees only part of its surroundings, while the route may extend beyond several intersections and turns. A locally feasible movement is therefore not necessarily useful for reaching the destination. As an example, the route in Figure 1 first requires approaching a crossing before continuing toward the goal. Thus, reliable navigation in such environment requires connecting such intermediate steps to the long-horizon goal while anticipating how local actions may affect subsequent progress. Recent language and vision-language models (VLMs) have enabled navigation agents to reason explicitly about instructions, observations, and possible actions (Zhou et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024; Ruan et al., 2026). Rather than directly executing a policy prediction, these agents use reasoning to assess where to move next. EvoNav (Dai et al., 2026) further incorporates historical experience and future thoughts by retrieving past experience and predicting subsequent actions and landmarks. These advances establish reasoning over experience as a practical way to improve navigation decisions.

Compared with reasoning from observed history and language-based future thoughts, PreAct-Nav maintains medium-horizon subgoals to decompose long-horizon navigation tasks and uses action-conditioned future visual cues to preview, review, and revise local actions before execution. Real feedback updates memory for subsequent decisions. Despite this progress, connecting local reasoning to long-horizon navigation remains challenging in two respects. First , historical context records past observations and actions, but provides limited guidance for subsequent movements toward the long-horizon goal. Local decisions may therefore be reasonable in isolation yet inconsistent across steps. Second , reasoning about future actions and landmarks in language does not provide visual evidence of what a proposed movement might reveal. A passage that appears plausible from the current view may remain occluded or fail to lead toward the intended route. These limitations make it difficult to determine whether a locally plausible action will support progress toward the destination. In this paper, we propose PreAct-Nav , a training-free agentic reasoning harness that equips frozen navigation policies with anticipatory reasoning. Our central idea is to maintain medium-horizon subgoals to guide local decisions, evaluate proposed actions using predicted visual outcomes before execution, and update navigation memory using execution feedback. A shared VLM reasoner uses the current subgoal, relevant experience, and visual predictions to decide whether to retain or revise the proposed action. This reasoning loop connects action evaluation and revision before execution with memory updates afterward, without updating model weights. Specifically, the PreAct-Nav harness organizes a VLM reasoner and two callable tools into a four-stage reasoning loop (Figure 2). During Action Preview , the reasoner reads Navigation Memory ,which maintains the current subgoal and relevant experience across decisions. If the available evidence is insufficient, it queries the Predictive Sandbox , which uses an action-conditioned world model (AC-WM) to generate future views for the proposed movement (Bar et al., 2025). During 

Action Review , the reasoner uses predicted visual cues to assess whether the proposed action makes progress toward the current subgoal. It retains a supported action or revises a contradicted one; a revised action returns to preview and review within a fixed reasoning budget. In Action Execute , the agent follows the accepted movement. Finally, Memory Update compares the expected cues with the next real observation, records the outcome, and updates subgoal progress for the next decision. By coordinating context, tool use, and execution feedback, the harness maintains medium-horizon guidance across local actions: prediction guides revision, and real feedback updates memory. We evaluate PreAct-Nav on UrbanNav with three navigation policies and two VLM reasoners. Our method achieves relative improvements of 6.71% in success rate (SR) and 4.19% in success weighted by path length (SPL) over the benchmark’s original policy. Experiments with other policies and reasoners further assess its applicability beyond a single backbone. Component ablations and analyses of individual decisions examine the roles of memory and prediction, with larger prediction gains on longer routes and routes with more turns. In summary, our contributions are: 

❶ We introduce PreAct-Nav, a training-free navigation harness that reviews and revises policy actions using memory and visual prediction before execution. 

❷ We develop Navigation Memory to maintain medium-horizon subgoals alongside observed experi-ence and update their progress through execution feedback. 2Preprint 

❸ We expose an action-conditioned world model as a Predictive Sandbox, providing future visual cues for action reasoning under the current subgoal. 

❹ We evaluate the harness across navigation policies and VLM reasoners, with component ablations and analyses of memory, prediction, and navigation difficulty. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Navigation reasoning. Vision-and-language navigation grounds instructions in observed scenes, from navigation graphs to continuous environments (Anderson et al., 2018b; Krantz et al., 2020). Learned policies translate observations and goals into local movements (Mei et al., 2026b; Sridhar et al., 2024; Hirose et al., 2026), while CityWalker learns urban navigation from web videos (Liu et al., 2025). LM-Nav composes pretrained models without fine-tuning (Shah et al., 2023), while CompassNav supervises feasible-action decisions (Li et al., 2026). Explicit reasoning supports instruction interpretation (Zhou et al., 2024; Qiao et al., 2025), multi-expert assessment (Long et al., 2024), and retrieved experience with future thoughts (Dai et al., 2026). ReAct and Inner Monologue interleave reasoning with feedback (Yao et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023), while Toolformer learns when and how to call tools (Schick et al., 2023). Our frozen reasoner coordinates memory and prediction through a harness to review and revise movements before execution. 

Agent memory. Memory enables agents to reuse experience without encoding every interaction in model weights. Reflexion stores verbal reflections (Shinn et al., 2023), REMem retrieves temporally grounded events (Shu et al., 2026), and M3-Agent builds multimodal episodic and semantic memo-ries (Long et al., 2026). Agentic Context Engineering incrementally updates contextual playbooks using execution feedback (Zhang et al., 2026). In navigation, DUET combines a growing topological map with local observations (Chen et al., 2022), and PanoNav retains exploration history to avoid repeated local deadlocks (Jin et al., 2026). Our Navigation Memory couples observed experience with an active medium-horizon subgoal that provides shared context for action review and revision. Execution feedback updates its objective and progress cues, so memory tracks both past outcomes and the current purpose of navigation. 

Action-conditioned world models. Video generation provides a visual representation of future plans: UniPi generates goal-conditioned videos and extracts control actions from them (Du et al., 2023). Action-conditioned world models instead predict observations for a specified movement. Navigation World Models use such predictions to plan paths or rank proposed trajectories (Bar et al., 2025), while Cosmos Policy fine-tunes video models for visuomotor control and planning (Kim et al., 2026). In computer-use agents, R-WoM grounds language-model simulations in retrieved tutorials (Mei et al., 2026a). Access to a world model, however, does not ensure effective use of its predictions (Qian et al., 2026). Our focus is how visual prediction informs navigation reasoning. The Predictive Sandbox exposes the world model as a callable tool; the VLM interprets predicted visual cues against the current subgoal to retain or revise a movement. Real observations subsequently establish progress and update memory, linking predictive reasoning to execution feedback. 

3 METHODOLOGY 

PreAct-Nav is a training-free agentic reasoning harness for a frozen navigation policy. It organizes a shared VLM reasoner R and two callable tools into a loop that previews, reviews, and revises actions before execution. Navigation Memory maintains a medium-horizon subgoal and observed experience; the Predictive Sandbox provides action-conditioned future visual cues. Real execution feedback updates memory for subsequent decisions. The Predictive Sandbox exposes an action-conditioned world model W as a callable tool. From the current observation and proposed action, it generates a short video, or predicted rollout , for review under the current subgoal. 

Action-conditioned prediction. Let C(ajt ) encode the proposed movement as the positions and viewing directions required by the world model. The tool query and returned rollout are 

Qjt = ( ot, C(ajt ), ℓ, ϵ ), bOjt = W(Qjt ) = ( bojt, 1, . . . , bojt,L ), (5) where ℓ is a scene-continuation prompt, ϵ controls generation randomness, and L counts predicted frames. Across revisions, the initial observation, prompt, and seed remain fixed; only the proposed movement changes. Appendix D specifies action encoding and generation settings. 

Visual evidence collection. The reasoner inspects the rollout and its action using the destination, observations, subgoal, and open question. It returns three fields (Figure 2): Prediction quality assesses coherence, movement consistency, and relevance; Rationale links visual cues to the action assessment; and Expected future cues specify changes to check after execution. The reasoner looks for progress toward the subgoal, not completion within a single rollout. Incoherent or inconclusive predictions defer to observed evidence. Tool records retain the conditioning action, rollout, and assessment for revision within the current decision. 3.4 AGENTIC REASONING HARNESS

4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate PreAct-Nav on UrbanNav (Mei et al., 2026b) and CityWalker (Liu et al., 2025), examine transfer across frozen policies, and ablate Navigation Memory and the Predictive Sandbox. Qualitative examples illustrate action revision and memory update. Appendices F and G report route-level analyses, reasoning diagnostics, and inference costs. 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Tasks and evaluation. UrbanNav (Mei et al., 2026b) provides first-person urban trajectories and language descriptions of destination views. We evaluate on its test-seen and test-unseen splits, with 4,179 and 4,634 episodes after filtering watermark-related descriptions. On CityWalker (Liu et al., 2025), we use 154 routes drawn from nine recordings, with each destination specified by its position relative to the agent. Both tasks use a three-metre success radius. UrbanNav allows 25 decisions per episode and CityWalker allows 100. Methods share the evaluation protocol within each task; Appendix D gives its action and observation rules.

SR and SPL measure task completion and path efficiency; the remaining metrics measure final goal distance, reference-path alignment, and whether the goal region is reached at any step. NE is in metres and all other metrics are percentages. Improvements are percentage-point differences computed before rounding. 4.3 POLICY TRANSFER 

Table 3 evaluates the harness with three frozen navigation policies on test-unseen. Beyond the UrbanNav policy, NoMaD gains 2.78 SR points and 2.76 SPL points, while OmniVLA gains 3.58 and 3.39 points. SDTW increases from 73.55% to 76.13% for NoMaD and from 79.98% to 83.33% for OmniVLA. OmniVLA improves on all six metrics; for NoMaD, OSR is unchanged and NE and nDTW remain nearly constant. NoMaD’s gain primarily reflects better completion and path efficiency. These results show that the harness improves navigation without changing policy weights. 4.4 ABLATION STUDY 

Component ablation. Table 4 evaluates the Reasoning Harness and its two callable tools: Naviga-tion Memory Tool and Predictive Sandbox Tool. Without either tool, the harness reviews and revises policy actions, reaching 80.56% SR and 76.74% SPL. Adding the Navigation Memory Tool raises SR to 82.37%, while adding only the Predictive Sandbox Tool yields 81.48%. The full method achieves the highest SR of 83.38%, the lowest NE, and the highest nDTW, SDTW, and OSR. Its SPL is also highest at 77.87%, versus 77.24% with the sandbox alone. 

Route length and turning. Figure 3 reports gains over the UrbanNav policy within each route-length and turning interval. The full method improves SR and reduces NE in every interval. Its SR gain reaches 9.60 points on 30–50 m routes and 7.92 points on routes with 360–540 degrees of cumulative turning. The largest NE reductions occur in the longest and most turning-intensive groups, although SR gains decrease in these extreme groups. 5 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

We introduced PreAct-Nav, a training-free agentic reasoning harness that reviews and revises naviga-tion actions before execution. Navigation Memory maintains medium-horizon subgoals to decompose long-horizon tasks, while the Predictive Sandbox provides action-conditioned future visual cues. A shared VLM uses this context to retain or revise policy actions, and real feedback updates memory. Experiments across frozen policies show improved navigation success, with greater prediction gains on longer routes and routes with more turns. The harness remains limited by visual prediction quality and cost. Generated videos can provide ambiguous or inconsistent cues, and video generation dominates inference cost. More reliable, faster predictions are needed to extend this loop to time-sensitive navigation.

Shared context. The destination text describes the view at the destination, not directions relative to the current agent. Structured responses. Vertical bars in the printed response formats enumerate valid alternatives; a response returns one value, not the entire list. UrbanNav produces eight local waypoints. The reasoner receives both their coordinates and a deterministic physical endpoint description, for example a displacement to the right and forward from the current camera. A constructed action uses cumulative ranges and bearings, which an adapter converts to waypoints. Invalid or low-confidence revisions fall back to the policy proposal. Historical action coordinates retain labels identifying their original source frame. NoMaD (Sridhar et al., 2024) and OmniVLA (Hirose et al., 2026) use policy-specific action conversion and operating settings. For a revised action, waypoint k is specified by displacement rk from the current origin and bearing 

βk relative to forward. The conversion is xk = rk sin βk and yk = rk cos βk, with distances in metres and angles in radians. The permitted action set is

D.3 PREDICTIVE SANDBOX 

The implementation uses the Diffusers Cosmos3OmniPipeline and 

CosmosActionCondition in the camera_pose domain. Each query receives the current image, a camera trajectory derived from the proposed movement, and a shared scene-continuation prompt, and returns 60 predicted frames in addition to the input frame. To construct the camera trajectory, we prepend the origin to the eight waypoints and linearly interpolate their positions to 61 poses along the full proposed path. Camera headings follow successive waypoint displacements and are interpolated after angle unwrapping, with the initial heading aligned with the current view. Adjacent poses yield 60 relative transformations, each encoded by three translation coordinates and a six-dimensional rotation representation. These indices parameterize the prediction, not physical execution time. Across revisions, the camera path changes while the image, text, and seed remain fixed. Generation uses seed 0, 30 diffusion steps, guidance 1.0, flow shift 10.0, the 480-resolution tier, ego view, no sound, and no system prompt. Query records retain the source observation, proposed movement, route question, generated video, and action assessment, linking each predicted rollout to its conditioning action. D.4 URBAN NAV EVALUATION 

The splits contain 4,179 test-seen and 4,634 test-unseen episodes. Each agent receives the destination description and egocentric observations and produces a sequence of local waypoints. All main comparisons use a maximum of 25 navigation decisions per episode and a three-metre success radius. Internal reasoning iterations and tool calls do not count as additional navigation decisions. D.5 CITY WALKER EVALUATION 

The additional evaluation uses 154 route windows from nine recordings (IDs 10–18). Goals are fixed route endpoints expressed as rightward and forward displacements in the current frame. The frozen CityWalker policy (Liu et al., 2025) receives five chronological RGB observations and coordinate context. All methods execute at most two metres along the proposed path per decision. Success requires reaching within three metres of the goal. Stationary transitions consume the 100-decision budget. Baselines use task-specific prompt adaptations. Path-length accounting is defined in Appendix E.
