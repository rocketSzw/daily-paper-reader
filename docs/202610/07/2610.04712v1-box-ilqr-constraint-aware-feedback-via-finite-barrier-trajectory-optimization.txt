# Box-iLQR: Constraint-Aware Feedback via Finite-Barrier Trajectory Optimization 

Abhijeet ∗ and Suman Chakravorty ∗

Abstract — Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) and Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) are attractive trajectory-optimization methods because their backward recur-sions provide both nominal trajectories and local time-varying feedback laws. Hard state and input constraints, however, can drive solutions toward constraint boundaries where little corrective authority remains during closed-loop execution. This paper presents Box-iLQR , a log-barrier iLQR method for box-constrained states and controls, with emphasis on the resulting feedback policy. Barrier gradients bias nominal trajectories away from constraint boundaries, while barrier curvature enters the Riccati recursion that determines the feed-back gains. We show that control-barrier curvature smoothly attenuates feedback as an actuator approaches saturation, while state-barrier curvature propagates backward through the value function to shape feedback before future state constraints are encountered. This motivates retaining a finite barrier rather than always driving the barrier parameter toward zero, thereby preserving state and actuator margin. Comparisons with CL-DDP and ALTRO are presented on pendulum, cart-pole, Acrobot, and car-parking problems. Monte Carlo experiments demonstrate improved closed-loop behavior with finite barriers, while a six-input MuJoCo fish example demonstrates data-driven Box-iLQR using dynamics Jacobians estimated from simulator rollouts. Box-iLQR achieves convergence comparable to, and in some cases better than, the baseline methods. More-over, the feedback policy obtained with a finite barrier ensures constraint satisfaction even under execution uncertainty. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Trajectory optimization is a fundamental tool for motion planning and control of nonlinear robotic systems. Among shooting-based methods, Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP) and the iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) are particularly attractive because they exploit the temporal structure of the optimal-control problem and produce a time-varying local feedback law together with the optimized nominal trajectory [1], [2], [3]. Modern robotics software and algorithms such as Crocoddyl further demonstrate the practical utility of Riccati-based optimal-control methods for complex nonlinear systems [4]. Among Riccati-based trajectory-optimization methods, we focus on iLQR rather than full DDP. Recent Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) interpretations show that iLQR provides a consistent quadratic approximation of the discrete optimal-control problem, while the additional dynamics-Hessian terms in DDP can produce indefinite local models and poor cost predictions away from a solution [5], [6]. This makes iLQR particularly suitable for the barrier-continuation setting considered here, where updates of the

barrier parameters repeatedly change the local objective and may move the optimization away from its current minimizer. The feedback returned by iLQR is not merely a numerical byproduct. A related line of work views nonlinear control and reinforcement learning as the search for a nominal maneuver together with a local feedback controller, rather than as the direct search for a high-dimensional global policy [7], [8], [3]. In this view, trajectory optimization determines the operating trajectory, while local perturbation dynamics determine the feedback correction around it. The central difficulty is that practical systems are con-strained. Actuators saturate, joint or workspace coordinates are bounded, and safety requirements can impose explicit limits on states. CL-DDP incorporates box-constrained con-trols through stagewise constrained quadratic subproblems [9]. Feasibility-driven DDP methods improve globalization and initialization behavior [10], while hybrid and projection methods offer alternative constrained formulations [11], [12], [13]. ALTRO combines an augmented-Lagrangian formu-lation with structure-exploiting trajectory optimization to address general nonlinear constraints [14]. Interior-point methods provide another important class of constrained op-timization techniques, including structured optimal-control solvers and interior-point variants of DDP [15], [16], [17], [18]. A key distinction of the primal log-barrier formulation used in Box-iLQR is that every accepted iterate remains strictly inside the feasible region. In contrast, augmented-Lagrangian methods may pass through infeasible intermedi-ate trajectories while progressively reducing the constraint residual. Primal-dual interior-point methods likewise do not require strict primal feasibility at every intermediate iteration [17], [16], [18]. Box-iLQR, therefore, preserves strict feasi-bility throughout the accepted optimization sequence, at the cost of requiring a strictly feasible initialization. Moreover, Box-iLQR offers a critical advantage for feedback-oriented deployment: the log-barrier curvature directly shapes the Riccati gains, ensuring closed-loop system satisfies the con-straints under uncertainty. Pure primal barrier methods, however, introduce an im-portant numerical issue. Wright showed that after reducing a barrier parameter, a Newton step can become strongly infeasible even when computed from an interior iterate [19]. Classical nonlinear optimization therefore combines barrier models with globalization mechanisms such as line search or trust regions [20]. Similar behavior is relevant to trajectory optimization because a control perturbation at one time instant affects all downstream states. This paper takes a complementary viewpoint: if the output arXiv:2610.04712v1 [math.OC] 3 Oct 2026 of trajectory optimization will be used directly as a feedback controller , driving the barrier parameter arbitrarily close to zero may not be desirable. At a hard-constrained optimum, a control can lie almost exactly at its saturation limit, leaving little authority for disturbance rejection in one direction. Likewise, a nominal state that approaches a path-constraint boundary has little geometric margin against execution un-certainty. Retaining a finite logarithmic barrier intentionally shifts the nominal solution into the feasible interior and simultaneously modifies the local curvature from which the feedback matrix is computed. The principal contributions of this work are:  

> •

A finite-barrier Box-iLQR formulation that handles si-multaneous state and control box constraints while pre-serving the standard local feedback policy parametriza-tion of iLQR/DDP leading to the structured backward-forward search.  

> •

A characterization of how barrier curvature shapes the feedback gains. As a constrained control approaches its bound, the corresponding row of the feedback matrix tends to zero. On the other hand, the state-barrier cur-vature propagates backward and modifies the feedback before the state constraint is reached thereby allowing for constraint satisfaction even in the presence of un-certainty.  

> •

A finite-barrier stopping rule for feedback deployment. Instead of driving the barrier parameter to zero, the optimization may terminate at a positive barrier value to retain state and actuator margins for closed-loop execution that ensures constraint satisfaction.  

> •

Numerical validation against CL-DDP and ALTRO, to-gether with a data-driven MuJoCo example in which the local dynamics are identified from simulator rollouts. Experiments show that Box-iLQR is competitive/ better than CL-DDP and ALTRO on nominal constrained ot-pimal control, while the finite-barrier feedback reduces observed state-constraint violations to zero in problems under uncertainty, which the competing methods cannot accomplish. For the baseline comparisons, ALTRO is im-plemented using the open-source Altro.jl package [14], while CL-DDP is implemented following the control-limited DDP formulation of Tassa et al. [9]. This paper presented Box-iLQR, a log-barrier iLQR method for box-constrained states and controls that preserves the standard backward–forward structure and local affine feedback policy of unconstrained iLQR. iLQR is chosen over full DDP because its local approximation provides more reliable descent behavior away from the optimum [6]. This motivates re-taining a finite barrier when the feedback law, rather than only the nominal constrained optimum, is the desired output. Unlike penalty-based constrained trajectory optimization, the log-barrier formulation maintains strict feasibility of ac-cepted iterates, while still returning the local feedback policy associated with the constrained trajectory. Across pendulum, cart-pole, Acrobot, and car-parking benchmarks, Box-iLQR produces competitive control-constrained solutions and directly handles the state-constrained cases considered here. For state-constrained car parking, both ALTRO and Box-iLQR reach the desired ter-minal configuration, with comparable nominal costs. Closed-loop cart-pole and car-parking experiments demonstrate the value of the barrier-modified feedback policy under execution uncertainty. Retaining a finite barrier improves closed-loop behavior in the cart-pole and car-parking example, while Box-iLQR feedback eliminates the state-constraint violations observed under open-loop car-parking execution in the tested Monte Carlo set. The six-input MuJoCo fish experiment further shows that the same constrained feedback framework can use local dynamics identified entirely from simulator rollouts. Future work will focus on automatically selecting finite barrier values using disturbance or covariance information, developing infeasible-start and receding-horizon variants, and validating the method on physical robotic platforms.
