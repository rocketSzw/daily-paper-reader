# AFD-CAMLs: Agile Force-Distribution-Aware Planning and Control for Cable-Suspended Aerial Multi-Lifting Systems 

Antreas Kourris, Sihao Sun ∗

Abstract — Multiple UAVs can cooperatively transport heavy payloads while controlling their position and orientation. Trajectory-based methods offer high agility while satisfying system constraints, but can produce uneven force distributions when the tension-to-wrench allocation is redundant or ill-conditioned, particularly under geometric mismatch and low-level tracking errors. We propose a hybrid planning-and-control framework to address this problem. A global planner generates payload trajectories and cable-force references by exploring the allocation null space under a prescribed internal-force setting. These references augment the cost of a centralized local planner, promoting feasible force distributions while generating trajectories for all UAVs. An admittance filter then compares the planned forces with onboard cable-tension estimates and adjusts the kinematic references to improve force tracking in degenerate or near-degenerate configurations. Simulations and experiments involving four to ten UAVs demonstrate more balanced tension distributions during both hovering and demanding agile maneuvers, without compromising agility or payload-tracking performance. 

I. INTRODUCTION Teams of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) can cooper-atively transport payloads beyond the capacity of a single vehicle [1], [2]. Adding UAVs increases lifting capacity but can make the cable-force distribution non-unique. Accurate payload-pose tracking therefore does not ensure that the load is shared safely and evenly among the vehicles. This distinction is especially important for cable-suspended sys-tems: the net force and moment on the payload determine its motion, whereas internal-force components can change the individual cable tensions without changing that net wrench. Excessive tension can saturate or destabilize a vehicle, while insufficient tension can cause cable slackness and invalidate the assumed system dynamics. Existing methods can broadly be divided into position- and force-based approaches. Quasi-static position-based methods determine UAV configurations from payload poses, cable geometry, and static equilibrium [1], [3]. These methods are effective for slow motion but neglect dynamic coupling and do not guarantee feasible cable tensions. Force-based meth-ods compute the required payload wrench from a dynamic model and allocate it among the cables [4], [5], [6], allow-ing secondary objectives such as tension or internal-force regulation [7]. Most retain a cascaded wrench-allocation and inner-loop structure whose time-scale separation limits agile operation. Their force commands also depend on nominal payload and cable parameters. Recent work introduced a trajectory-based approach that replaces the conventional cascaded force controller with a centralized whole-body nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) planner and onboard trajectory-tracking controllers [9], [8]. The planner predicts the coupled load-cable dy-namics and generates dynamically feasible trajectories for all UAVs while enforcing thrust, cable-tautness, collision-avoidance, and obstacle-avoidance constraints. Agility is improved because the onboard controllers track complete kinematic references rather than realizing instantaneous force commands through separate control layers. This formulation also allows the planner to anticipate coupled payload and cable motion over a finite horizon instead of allocating a wrench independently at each instant. For four or more UAVs, however, multiple cable-force distributions can produce the same payload wrench. The planner generates kinematic trajectories from a nominal geometric model, and the low-level controllers do not di-rectly enforce the corresponding tensions. Attachment-point and cable-length errors, combined with trajectory-tracking errors, can therefore shift the physical system toward a different force equilibrium. The payload may still track accurately while some UAVs become overloaded and other cables approach slackness. Improving payload-pose tracking alone cannot correct this internal-force component because it does not affect the resultant payload wrench. The problem becomes most pronounced when the tension-to-wrench al-location matrix is degenerate or nearly degenerate, because small geometric or tracking errors can then produce large changes in the individual tensions. A practical solution must therefore select a feasible distribution during planning and arXiv:2610.01185v1 [cs.RO] 1 Oct 2026 regulate its realization without sacrificing the agility of the trajectory-based architecture. This work extends the trajectory-based framework [8] with explicit planning and feedback regulation of the cable-force distribution. An internal-force-aware global planner explores the null space of the payload wrench-allocation matrix to generate feasible cable-force and direction references. These references are incorporated into the centralized NMPC cost so that the locally planned trajectories account for the desired force distribution. Because the cable forces are still realized indirectly through vehicle motion, an admittance filter com-pares each planned tension with an onboard estimate and converts the error into position, velocity, and acceleration corrections. The resulting hybrid motion–force architecture combines predictive whole-body planning with local force feedback. The three main contributions are: 1) an internal-force-aware global planner that preserves the required payload wrench while generating tension-feasible cable-force and direction references through null-space optimization; 2) a force-reference-aware NMPC formulation and an admittance filter that uses onboard tension estimates to regulate the realized force distribution through local kinematic corrections; and 3) simulation studies with four to ten UAVs and real-world experiments with four UAVs (Figure 1) that demonstrate improved force distribution under geomet-ric and inertial-model mismatch while preserving agile payload tracking. M ETHODOLOGY 

The proposed framework augments the trajectory-based architecture of [8] with explicit force-distribution planning and local force feedback. As summarized in Fig. 2, an internal-force-aware global planner first generates a reference trajectory for the payload and cables. A centralized NMPC local planner then converts these references into dynamically feasible, constraint-satisfying trajectories for the complete multi-lifting system. Relative to [8], the system model, constraints, solver, and trajectory decoder are retained, while the NMPC objective is augmented with the cable-force and cable-direction references supplied by the global planner. Finally, an admittance filter uses the discrepancy between the planned and observed cable tensions to modify the kinematic reference of each quadrotor. This hybrid motion–force struc-ture preserves the agility of trajectory-based control while regulating the force distribution that can otherwise become indeterminate for systems with four or more quadrotors.

D. Onboard trajectory tracking 

The corrected references are tracked by the same flatness-based PD and INDI controller used in [8]. The flatness mapping generates the required attitude and feedforward quantities, while INDI rejects unmodeled force and torque disturbances using onboard inertial measurements [12], [13]. This component is unchanged in the present work; its role is to execute the admittance-corrected local trajectory and generate the rotor commands. IV. R ESULTS

to 2.09 N .V. C ONCLUSIONS 

This paper presented a hybrid planning-and-control frame-work for improving cable-force distribution in trajectory-based aerial multi-lifting systems with four or more UAVs. An internal-force-aware global planner exploits the null space of the payload wrench-allocation matrix to gener-ate feasible cable-force and direction references, which are incorporated into the cost of a centralized NMPC local planner. Because the resulting forces are realized indirectly through kinematic UAV trajectories, an admittance filter further adjusts the position, velocity, and acceleration ref-erences using onboard cable-tension estimates. Simulation studies with teams of up to ten UAVs and experiments with four UAVs demonstrate that the proposed approach reduces tension imbalance and improves robustness to geometric model mismatch while preserving accurate payload tracking during both hovering and agile flight. Future work will use the inverse condition number k(A) to tune the admittance parameters online, enabling stronger tension correction near degenerate configurations and less intervention when the allocation matrix is well conditioned.
