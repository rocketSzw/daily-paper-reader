# Towards Agile Vision-Based Multi-UAV Flight: Revisiting State Estimation 

Michal Pliska, Matouˇ s Vrba, Ondˇ rej V´ ıta, Martin Jirouˇ sek, Viktor Walter and Martin Saska 

Abstract —Agile multi-UAV flight requires accurate and low-latency onboard estimation of the kinematic states of neighboring UAVs for collision avoidance, motion coordination, etc. Most vision-based approaches rely on position-only measurements, inferring velocity and acceleration indirectly from displacement. We show that this introduces a fixed structural delay in the estimation of higher-order states, which limits the achievable agility. To address this, we propose to integrate tilt measurements, provided by a state-of-the-art visual detector, which inform about the thrust direction of co-planar multirotor UAVs. We benchmark four position-only and five pose-aware estimators, including a novel formulation of a linear thrust-constraining Kalman Filter, on two real-world and one high-fidelity photorealistic simulated dataset over different levels of agility (3–21 m s −2 ). In our setup, pose-aware estimation consistently reduces the average velocity and acceleration estimation errors by 40 % and 57 % across the three datasets with the proposed KF formulation outperforming the other estimators. Position-only filters exhibit a constant ∼300 ms delay in acceleration step response independent of agility, whereas the tilt-constrained estimators operate near the physical response limit given by the camera frame-rate by observing the change in thrust direction before the displacement accumulates. In a closed-loop leader– follower simulated experiment with NMPC control, position-only estimation of the leader’s state fails to facilitate stable hovering of the follower, while the proposed estimator enables tracking of lateral maneuvers exceeding 2 g of acceleration.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent advancements in multirotor Unmanned Aerial Ve-hicle (UAV) control have pushed the agility of individual autonomous UAVs to the edge of the platforms’ capabilities, surpassing even expert human pilots [1]–[3]. However, state-of-the-art multi-robot UAV systems lag significantly behind these advances in agility. This is especially true for multi-UAV systems that rely on onboard relative localization to remove the requirement of intra-robot communication, which introduces non-negligible measurement noise [4], [5]. While the problem has been extensively studied [6]–[8], the reported experiments are typically conducted at velocities and accelerations that remain well below the agility routinely achieved in single-UAV scenarios with full state knowledge. In this work, we present the building blocks necessary to fill this research gap.

When considering multirotor UAV dynamics, direction of the thrust vector provides useful information about an ob-served target’s state and is often directly measurable, whether through the vehicle’s attitude, propeller orientation, or other visual cues. For a typical multirotor platform with co-planar propellers, the thrust vector is aligned with the direction of the propellers. There are several works that propose methods to exploit this feature of multirotor dynamics using visual detection of the full 6D pose to measure the UAV’s position and attitude [9]–[11]. Despite this growing recognition, it remains unknown to what extent including the thrust direction measurement improves state estimation over position-only methods. In particular, no systematic comparison across agility regimes with consistent tuning procedures and explicit finite-time latency analysis exists. This paper addresses this gap and presents the first sys-tematic comparison of pose-aware and position-only multi-rotor UAV state estimation, conducted over a full monoc-ular perception-to-estimation pipeline across real-world and simulated datasets spanning multiple agility levels. The best position-only and pose-aware methods are also compared in an agile leader-follower scenario running the whole pipeline from UAV control, onboard visual detection and state estimation of the leader, to the formation control (see Fig. 1). These experiments demonstrate that the pose-aware relative state es-timation is a necessary element for realizing truly agile multi-UAV motion coordination approaching the dynamic limits of individual UAVs. arXiv:2609.39611v1 [cs.RO] 30 Sep 2026 2 IEEE/RSJ IROS 2026. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Onboard UAV detection 

Classical approaches of onboard detection of other UAVs rely on various kinds of markers, such as LEDs placed onto the targets to be detected and localized [5], which enables reliably extracting their full poses without relying on domain-specific training. A distinct subset of UAV detection methods relies on sensors such as stereo cameras or LiDARs to detect flying UAVs using the spatial information provided by these sensors [12], [13]. This approach inherently yields the relative 3D position of the target, but requires equipping the observer UAV with specialized, potentially heavy, and expensive sen-sors. Recently, deep learning-based methods became sufficiently robust, accurate, and computationally efficient for deployment onboard UAVs with limited payload [14], [15]. Modern deep-learning detectors can extract additional information about the target in addition to its relative 3D position, such as the full 6D pose, using cheap and lightweight monocular RGB cameras and without relying on markers [9]–[11]. At the propeller level, ˇSpetl´ ık et al. [16] proposed a method for real-time roll and pitch estimation from event-camera streams. Although similar event-based approaches have so far only been demonstrated in controlled laboratory conditions, they have the potential to offer a complementary sensing modality to RGB-based pose estimation. In this work, we utilize the YOLOv5-6D deep learning architecture [17] that relies on visual keypoint detection, and Perspective-n-Point (PnP) to obtain full 6D poses of the detected objects assuming their known dimensions. 

B. UAV tracking and state estimation 

State estimation of aerial targets is most often formulated through position-based Kalman Filters (KFs) using simple kinematic models, most commonly Constant Velocity (CV) or Constant Acceleration (CA) [13]–[15], [18]. These models assume smooth motion and compensate for any unmodeled dynamics with process noise, which is typically tuned em-pirically. While sufficient for moderately maneuvering targets (max. acceleration reported in [13]–[15], [18] was 4.5 m s −2 ), this approach introduces a fundamental trade-off: low process noise yields estimation lag, whereas high noise produces jitter and instability [19]. Crucially, since these approaches rely exclusively on position measurements, uncertainty propagates and amplifies into higher-order state estimates. Several recent works have begun to incorporate attitude information into UAV state estimation. The authors of [9] proposed a custom convolutional neural network for extracting salient keypoints of a co-planar UAV combined with the standard PnP algorithm for full pose estimation, and a CA-KF relying on an assumption of zero vertical acceleration of the observed target. A very similar approach was described in [10], but with a more general Pseudo-Linear Kalman Filter (PLKF)-based estimator. [10] also publishes a dataset for UAV pose detection obtained in laboratory conditions with motion capture ground truth, which we utilize in this work for baseline comparison. A complementary approach is presented in [11], removing the assumption of known physical dimensions of the tracked target, and providing an observability analysis of the problem. However, all these works evaluate their methods on scenar-ios with limited dynamic excitation of the target and negligible vertical motion, which does not sufficiently cover the intended application of agile multi-robot motion. These works also do not report the method used for tuning the estimation parameters. 

C. Contributions 

The presented paper provides a systematic comparison of position-only and pose-aware vision-based state estimation of co-planar multirotor UAVs. We compare performance across different agility levels using identical automated tuning for all estimators to ensure fairness. To facilitate this, we re-lease a new UAV dataset created in high-fidelity simulations containing diverse maneuvers of varying agility. Furthermore, we propose the Degenerate Kalman Filter (DKF) framework for formulating a linear KF with general dynamic affine subspace measurements of the state vector, which is a common problem in robotics [10], [11], [20]. Using this framework, we derive the Z-axis measurement Kalman Filter (Z-KF) – an efficient linear KF variant for the task of pose-aware UAV state estimation, which outperforms other state-of-the-art methods. Finally, we demonstrate a simulated experiment running the full pipeline, including UAV control, onboard perception, state estimation, and trajectory prediction of the target in a leader-follower scenario with high agility (leader reaching velocities of 14 m s −1 and accelerations of 20 m s −2 ). We believe that our work will serve as a springboard for other researchers aiming to push their multi-UAV systems from idealized simulations and laboratories to challenging real-world conditions. In the context of the state of the art, we summarize our contributions as follows: 1) The first systematic comparison of pose-aware and position-only state estimation for vision-based UAV tracking is provided, evaluated through a full monocular perception-to-estimation pipeline over different levels of agility w.r.t. the target’s acceleration. 2) A novel linear KF formulation that incorporates pose as a directional acceleration constraint for improved UAV state estimation is formally derived using the Degenerate Kalman Filter (DKF) framework. 3) A benchmark dataset with photorealistic rendering and exact ground truth across seven agility levels is released, enabling reproducible comparison of estimators for agile full-pose vision-based UAV state estimation. 4) The impact of the state estimation on communication-free UAV motion coordination is evaluated in a simu-lated closed-loop leader-follower scenario, demonstrat-ing that the proposed pose-aware estimation enables reaching unprecedented agile levels of multi-UAV flight. Similarly to the position-only KFs in sec. III-A, we also evaluate both the analytical and block-diagonal process noise matrix for the Z-KF. IV. D ATASET 

As the research stream explored in this paper is relatively fresh, the number of publicly available benchmarking datasets is low. To the best of our knowledge, the only relevant dataset was published in [10], which contains RGB images of a flying UAV captured by a stationary camera in laboratory conditions with ground truth poses from motion capture. We split this dataset based on the model of the observed UAV (Phantom 4 and Mavic 2), and use it for baseline evaluation. However, this dataset does not provide higher-order states, which must therefore be derived retrospectively, introducing potential inaccuracies, and the motion range and agility of the target are limited (especially in the vertical direction). To address this, we release a new dataset created using the Unreal Engine-based simulator FlightForge [22], which offers photorealistic rendering and high physical fidelity with full UAV dynamics. The dataset consists of images from a moving camera observing a target UAV following trajectories generated using the method proposed in [19] with varying levels of agility in all axes (3–21 m s −2 ) and at different ranges (1.5–8 m). This allows us to evaluate the compared methods w.r.t. different flight modes and agility levels of the target. To achieve maximal fidelity of the observed states, a full UAV control pipeline from [23] is used to track these trajectories. Exact ground-truth 3D bounding boxes for detector training, full 6D pose, velocity, and acceleration are available directly from the simulator, eliminating the need for numerical differ-entiation of higher-order states. B. Compared Methods 

We compare the proposed Z-KF estimator with the baseline CV and CA KFs described in sec. III-A, and with relevant state-of-the-art approaches. Specifically, we include the Pose-KF estimator proposed in [10], which uses a CV model and incorporates the tilt measurements through the process noise covariance matrix, and the Multiplicative Extended Kalman Filter (MEKF), which is a widely adopted method for UAV ego-state estimation. For the MEKF, we modify the standard formulation (see e.g. [24]) to correspond to the problem considered in this paper. Specifically, we use a nominal state vector x = p⊺ v⊺ q⊺⊺, where q is a quaternion representing the UAV’s orientation, and a measurement vector 

z = p⊺ q⊺⊺ + ζpq , where ζpq is the measurement noise. We consider both the analytical and BDC formulations of the process noise covariance matrix of the MEKF, as described in sec. III-A. Because the Pose-KF utilizes this matrix for fusion of the tilt measurement, it cannot easily be modified in the same manner, so we follow the original formulation.

G. Closed-Loop Validation 

To demonstrate the system-level impact of estimation qual-ity, a leader-follower scenario is evaluated in a closed-loop experiment using a high-fidelity photorealistic simulator [22]. The leader UAV flies along the y-axis between increasingly distant waypoints, reaching accelerations above 20 m s −2 . The follower, offset by 4 m in x, observes the leader through a 110° FOV camera using the full perception pipeline (YOLOv5-6D, PnP, state estimator). The estimated state is propagated over a 1.4 s horizon, matching the NMPC control horizon [1]. The estimator parameters are re-tuned on MEN error over the predicted trajectory, aligning the objective with the control task. We compare the Z-KF and CA-KF. When the CA-KF is employed, the follower becomes unstable already during hovering, losing the leader within approximately 5 s. Estimation errors compound over the 1.4 s prediction horizon, causing progressively larger control cor-rections until the leader exits the camera field of view. Both variants (CA-KF, CA-KF+BDC) exhibit the same failure. With Z-KF, the follower maintains stable hovering and subsequently tracks the leader through lateral maneuvers above 2 g (up to 60° tilt, 14 m s −1 ) over a 20 s sequence (see Fig. 5). The follower itself reaches accelerations above 25 m s −2 due to corrective control. VI. D ISCUSSION & C ONCLUSION 

This work provided a detailed analysis of the influence of including thrust-direction information in vision-based state estimation of multirotor UAVs over using only position mea-surements. The main limitation of position-only estimation is structural: higher-order states must be inferred from ac-cumulated displacement. This problem manifests already at low agility levels, substantially affecting estimation accuracy. Moreover, it introduces an inherent estimation lag, bound-ing the achievable agility when used in closed-loop control. Incorporating the thrust direction significantly reduces the estimation error and delay. Although we used real-world datasets and high-fidelity sim-ulation to ensure robust evaluation, several limitations remain. Our PnP-based pose extraction assumes known dimensions of the target, which may be limiting in some scenarios, but this can be solved e.g. by utilizing a stereo camera for distance measurement. The closed-loop experiments were conducted only in simulation, with real-world validation remaining an open problem, which we plan to address in future work. Overall, the presented results demonstrate that to achieve truly agile communication-free multi-UAV flight, conventional state estimation approaches based on relative localization are insufficient.
