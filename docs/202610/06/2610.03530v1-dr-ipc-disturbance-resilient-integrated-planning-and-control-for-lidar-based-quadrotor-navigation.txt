DR-IPC: Disturbance-Resilient Integrated Planning
and Control for LiDAR-Based Quadrotor Navigation
Peng Liu1, Jingyan Wang1, Qipeng Ye2, Wen Li1, Jinya Su1, Senior Member, IEEE,
Zuo Wang1, Member, IEEE, Shihua Li1, Fellow, IEEE, Yunda Yan3, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—LiDAR-based quadrotor navigation in cluttered
environments
remains
challenging
under
external
distur-
bances, particularly when obstacle-aware motion generation and
disturbance-rejection control are handled in separate layers. This
article presents disturbance-resilient integrated planning and
control (DR-IPC), which combines lightweight path guidance
with nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) to directly
generate angular velocity and thrust. An interconnected extended
Kalman filter and nonlinear disturbance observer jointly provide
filtered state estimates and reconstructed disturbances for NMPC
prediction. The resulting formulation unifies nonlinear quadrotor
dynamics, actuator constraints, local motion generation, and pe-
nalized safe-flight-corridor residuals without requiring a separate
trajectory-optimization stage. Gazebo and MARSIM simulations,
together with indoor and outdoor experiments, validate DR-IPC
under wind, suspended payloads, narrow passages, ball impacts
and reactive avoidance of a dynamic obstacle. In multi-goal
navigation with disturbances, DR-IPC increases the number of
completed missions from 1/10 to 9/10 in Gazebo and reduces the
altitude RMSE from 0.34 to 0.01 m in experiments. The complete
system operates onboard at 100 Hz. Supplementary videos are
available on the project page https://drpp316.github.io/DR-IPC
-Page/, and the source code will be released.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Obstacle-Aware Motion Generation and Predictive Control
1) Hierarchical
Planning
and
Control:
Conventional
quadrotor navigation separates path planning, trajectory op-
timization, and tracking control. A front-end planner typically
uses a geometric or simplified point-mass model, while a
back-end optimizer converts the path into a smooth nominal
trajectory. Representative methods include SFC-based polyno-
mial optimization [13], minimum-snap generation [14], EGO-
Planner [9], and hierarchical two-layer MPC [15]. A lower-
level feedback controller then tracks the optimized trajectory.
In this architecture, obstacle avoidance is handled only
by the planner, typically using a simplified model without
disturbance effects. The resulting path may be geometrically
collision-free but not dynamically feasible under disturbances.
At the control level, as illustrated in Fig. 3(a), the tracking
controller does not have obstacle information and continues
to reduce the tracking error after the vehicle deviates from the
reference. It may drive vehicles toward nearby obstacles with-
out timely replanning. Consequently, the performance of HPC
may degrade in cluttered environments under disturbances.
2) Integrated Planning and Control: Integrated schemes
have attracted increasing attention [1], [8]. It acts as a local
predictive planner-controller that uses global guidance and
obstacle information [11]. At each receding-horizon update,
it jointly optimizes collision-aware local motion and control
inputs, rather than merely tracking a prescribed trajectory
without accounting for obstacles [25]. As shown in Fig. 3(b),
when wind drives the vehicle away from the reference, the
integrated schemes can adjust both the local motion around
the obstacle and the corresponding control action.
Existing integrated methods couple motion generation and
control in different ways. Obstacle-aware NMPC [16] imposes
a prescribed UAV–obstacle distance as a hard constraint. CBF-
based MPC [17] embeds forward-invariant safety conditions

B. Robust and Disturbance-aware MPC
1) Robust MPC: Robust MPC handles bounded distur-
bances and uncertainties without explicitly reconstructing
them. Robust visual-servo NMPC [19] maintains target vis-
ibility through constraint tightening. Efficient NMPC [20]
converts inequality constraints into cost penalties and imposes
a contraction constraint for real-time stable tracking.
2) Disturbance-aware MPC: Disturbance observer (DOB)-
based
quadrotor
controllers
have
been
developed
for
disturbance-rejection tracking and landing [23], [24], fol-
lowing the general disturbance-estimation and compensation
framework [26], [27]. Observer-based MPC further incor-
porates disturbance estimates into prediction. DA-MPC [21]
injects an NDO estimate into the prediction model. DR-MPC
[22] combines a generalized extended-state observer with
predictive visual servoing for UAV pipeline inspection.
Both branches employ MPC for prescribed trajectory or
visual-target tracking. Robust MPC does not reconstruct the
disturbance, whereas disturbance-aware MPC incorporates its
estimate into prediction. However, neither couples disturbance
rejection with obstacle-aware local motion generation.
C. Positioning of DR-IPC
As summarized in Table I, existing obstacle-aware meth-
ods generally omit online disturbance reconstruction, whereas
disturbance-aware MPC methods, which mainly serve as low-
level control strategies, do not include obstacle information in
prediction. The proposed DR-IPC bridges this gap by integrat-
ing disturbance reconstruction with obstacle-aware predictive
control. Building on our previous linear KF+DOB architecture
[28], the interconnected observer is extended to nonlinear
quaternion dynamics. The resulting EKF+NDO supplies fil-
tered states and reconstructed disturbances to an obstacle-
aware NMPC that jointly optimizes local motion, angular
velocity and thrust. Without a separate trajectory-optimization
stage, the onboard system supports LiDAR-based navigation
and suspended-payload transportation in disturbance-rich, clut-
tered outdoor and factory environments.

C. Problem Statement
At update k, DR-IPC receives the noisy odometry mea-
surement yk, the A*-based geometric guidance Rk, and the
local SFC Ck. Since the true state xk and lumped disturbance
dk are unavailable, online state filtering and disturbance
reconstruction are required for predictive control.
Sudden disturbances or map updates may drive the motion
outside the SFC. A hard SFC constraint may make the
optimal control problem (OCP) infeasible or induce aggressive
recovery and oscillatory flight. The challenge is therefore to
use a soft penalty for SFC violations while maintaining OCP
feasibility under disturbances [20].
Accordingly, the objective is to jointly optimize dynamically
feasible local motion and the angular velocity/thrust using the
filtered state and reconstructed disturbance, while considering
nonlinear dynamics, soft SFC penalties, and input constraints.

C. Multi-Goal Navigation
Three configurations are evaluated in Gazebo: IPC without
disturbance, IPC with disturbance, and DR-IPC with distur-
bance. IPC without disturbances establishes nominal task fea-
sibility, whereas IPC and DR-IPC are compared under identi-
cal disturbed conditions to evaluate disturbance resilience. For
the disturbed cases, the quadrotor is subjected to both constant
wind and suspended-payload disturbances. Each configuration
is evaluated over 10 trials in the environment shown in Fig. 9.
Each trial requires the vehicle to reach 4 goals sequentially,
yielding 4×10 prescribed goals for each configuration. A goal
is counted as reached when its horizontal distance is below
0.8 m, and a mission is completed after all four goals are
reached. Minor collision and fall events are recorded and do
not terminate a trial if the vehicle can continue the mission.
As shown in Table V, IPC achieves full mission completion
without disturbances but degrades markedly under wind and
payload effects, completing only 1 of 10 missions and 9 of
40 goals. In contrast, DR-IPC completes 9 of 10 missions
and reaches 37 of 40 goals under the same disturbances,
while reducing average collision/fall events from 5 to 0.1
per completed mission. This comparison shows that DR-IPC
maintains higher mission completion with fewer collision and
fall events under combined wind and payload disturbances.

D. Outdoor Experiments
Four field scenarios are considered: daytime and nighttime
flights in the same wooded area, and suspended-payload
delivery along two factory routes, as shown in Fig. 1. The


wooded site contains dense vegetation, irregular branches, and
narrow clearances, while nighttime operation provides limited
visual cues. In the factory, machinery and stored materials
restrict ground access and wheeled transport, motivating aerial
delivery of lightweight payloads.
Fig. 13 shows the LiDAR point-cloud maps and executed
trajectories for the two factory routes. The quadrotor transports
a suspended payload from the loading point to the delivery
point while avoiding columns, equipment, and stored materi-
als. Throughout the four routes, LiDAR-based odometry and
mapping, A* planning, SFC construction, EKF+NDO estima-
tion, and NMPC operate online. Despite ambient airflow and
payload-induced disturbances, DR-IPC completes all routes
without collision.
VII. CONCLUSIONS
This paper proposes DR-IPC for LiDAR-based quadrotor
navigation. The nonlinear interconnected EKF+NDO sup-
presses noise and improves the accuracy and smoothness of
disturbance estimation, thereby reducing fluctuations in the
control commands and attitude response. To accommodate
disturbance-induced deviations, the soft SFC penalizes cor-
ridor violations while avoiding excessive corrective responses.
Together with an obstacle- and disturbance-aware NMPC
backend that jointly optimizes local motion and angular-
velocity/thrust commands, these components enable DR-IPC
to operate in cluttered, noisy, and disturbance-rich environ-
ments. Comparative simulations and experiments demonstrate
improved navigation reliability and disturbance rejection over
IPC, while wooded and factory flights verify onboard opera-
tion in unstructured environments.
