Abstract 

Full actuation is commonly valued for independent position and attitude tracking. This work establishes a complementary principle: when only a position trajectory is prescribed, minimizing a positively homogeneous allocation cost selects underactuated-like behavior on a fully actuated multirotor. This cost class includes norms and power laws, including standard minimum-norm pseudoinverse allocation. We use fiberwise infimization to transport the actuator-output cost to wrench space, yielding a homogeneous infimal allocation cost. Restriction to unit zero-moment forces defines a directional landscape whose minimizing directions are independent of force magnitude. We show that aligning any fixed minimizing direction with the required world-frame force minimizes instantaneous and integrated zero-moment allocation costs. The resulting body force remains in a fixed one-dimensional subspace without transverse components. We derive an exact optimality-gap decomposition quantifying the additional cost of every other attitude trajectory and characterizing the minimum-cost class. When a disturbance or prescribed-motion change modifies the required-force direction, a fully actuated platform can immediately generate the modified force using transverse authority and reorient toward the new minimum-cost family. A structurally underactuated platform must reorient before generating that force. Thus, homogeneous allocation-cost minimization explains why optimal nominal motion can be underactuated-like, while full actuation remains valuable for disturbance rejection and transient force generation. 

Keywords: Control allocation, emergent underactuation, fully actuated multirotors, homogeneous allocation costs. 

## 1 Introduction 

Conventional parallel-axis multirotors generate force along one body-fixed direction and must reorient it to accelerate laterally. Fully actuated multirotors remove this structural coupling by generating a complete body wrench indepen-dently of attitude, enabling independent pose control and physical interaction [1], [2]. Representative architectures use bidirectional spatial thrust arrangements [3], [4], uni-directional tilted propellers [5], active tilting mechanisms [6], [7], or reconfigurable structures [8], [9]. This actuation freedom is particularly valuable in aerial manipulation and contact inspection, where force application cannot generally be subordinated to attitude motion [10], [11]. In these platforms the control-allocation layer distributes a requested wrench among the available actuators. Redundant actuation supports constraints, failure accommodation, and secondary optimization [12], [13]. For multirotors, allocation geometry determines force–moment coupling and failure tolerance [14], while optimal allocation for prescribed wrenches has also been considered [15]. Standard homo-geneous costs include norms and power laws, notably the minimum-norm cost underlying pseudoinverse allocation. A different redundancy problem arises when position is prescribed and attitude remains free. Position determines the required world-frame force, whereas attitude selects its body-frame direction. The directionally nonuniform actuation of the platform therefore assigns different infimal allocation costs to attitudes realizing the same translation. This work shows that minimizing a positively homoge-neous allocation cost selects underactuated-like behavior on a fully actuated multirotor. Aligning any fixed minimizing direction with the required world-frame force confines the body force to a fixed one-dimensional subspace without transverse components. Full actuation remains valuable when disturbances or motion changes modify the required-force direction: transverse authority generates the new force immediately while reorientation recovers minimum-cost alignment. Thus, nominal efficiency favors underactuated-like motion, while full actuation supports tracking resilience. The main contributions of this study are: 1) a fiberwise infimal-cost formulation showing that pos-itively homogeneous allocation costs induce force-magnitude-independent minimizing directions; 2) a characterization of the minimum-cost attitude family, emergent underactuation, and the exact optimality-gap ordering of full-pose trajectories realizing the same position motion; and 3) a parallel-thrust lower bound and disturbance-response interpretation clarifying the efficiency and tracking roles of underactuated and fully actuated force generation. The logical structure of this work is summarized in Fig. 1. The map separates the allocation and homogeneity foundations from the core magnitude–direction result, its underactuated-like consequence, the full-pose ordering, reserve wrench authority, and the numerical illustration. 

## 2 Related Work 

Multirotor modeling and control have developed from platform-specific dynamic models and near-hover stabiliza-tion [16], [17] toward nonlinear and geometric control on SO (3) and SE (3) [18], [19]. Differential flatness and polyno-mial trajectory generation have enabled dynamically feasible and aggressive flight [20], [21], while efficient motion primitives and aerodynamic feedforward have extended these capabilities to online planning and high-speed tracking [22], [23]. These approaches exploit the underactuated structure of conventional parallel-thrust multirotors, for which translation determines a preferred thrust direction and attitude orients that direction in space. Incremental nonlinear dynamic inversion provides high-performance attitude control with online adaptation of control effectiveness [24], and its com-bination with structured robust control supports disturbance rejection and full-pose tracking of redundantly actuated multirotors [25]. Multirotor co-design has addressed robust performance, handling qualities, and motor failures jointly [26], whereas bidirectional fixed-pitch propulsion has been studied through unified modeling, nonlinear control, and control allocation [27]. These developments show how actuation design and feedback control jointly determine the usable wrench authority of a multirotor. A complementary research line changes the actua-tion geometry itself. Tilted, vectored, bidirectional, and reconfigurable rotor arrangements provide complete or configuration-dependent wrench authority [1], [3]–[9], [28]– [30]. Allocation-based classifications clarify how rotor 2number, placement, orientation, and tilting mechanisms determine attainable wrenches and platform capabilities [2], [14], [31]. At the control level, intrinsic tracking on homogeneous Riemannian manifolds provides a unified treatment of fully actuated mechanical systems that includes omnidirectional aerial robots [32]. This literature mainly ex-ploits full actuation for independent pose tracking, enhanced wrench generation, or configuration-specific objectives. This work instead investigates which attitude is selected when only translation is prescribed and allocation cost determines the use of the remaining actuation freedom. Redundant actuation and fault tolerance provide a further connection between vehicle geometry and allocation. Gen-eral control-allocation methods distribute commanded inputs while accommodating actuator constraints and secondary objectives [12], [13]. For multirotors, allocation has been studied through force–moment coupling, control authority, failure tolerance, and energy-aware wrench realization [14], [15], [33]. Relaxed-hover solutions show how attitude and residual rotor authority can sustain flight after severe actuator loss [34], while controllability analyses characterize the authority retained under rotor degradation and failure [35]. These studies assess or optimize allocation for a specified wrench, failure condition, or operating regime. The redundancy considered here additionally includes the attitude freedom that maps one prescribed world-frame force into different body-frame allocation demands. This distinction is especially relevant to aerial manipu-lation and physical interaction. Early aerial manipulators combined multirotors with robotic mechanisms and interac-tion controllers [36], [37]. Compliance and impact-absorbing mechanisms subsequently improved contact robustness [38], while conventional underactuated platforms were shown capable of applying sustained forces through coordinated attitude and contact control [39]. Fully actuated platforms permit contact force and vehicle orientation to be regulated more independently, as demonstrated for omnidirectional contact inspection [11], [40]. Reviews of aerial manipulation and multirotor physical interaction document the resulting diversity of platforms, mechanisms, controllers, and tasks [10], [41], [42]. Across these research directions, actuation geometry is commonly evaluated through wrench feasibility, tracking performance, robustness, or the cost of realizing a prescribed wrench. The question considered here is different: for a prescribed position motion and free attitude, which full-pose realization minimizes allocation cost? The fiberwise formulation shows that positive homogeneity makes the minimizing directions independent of force magnitude and can select a fixed body-force direction, thereby recovering an underactuated-like nominal realization from a fully actuated architecture.

## 8 Numerical Illustration 

We use a planar restriction of the three-dimensional model to compare three realizations of the same horizontal position trajectory: a parallel-thrust underactuated platform (UA), a fully actuated platform rotating toward the instantaneous minimum-allocation attitude (FA-opt), and a fully actu-ated platform maintaining the constant nonoptimal pitch 20 deg (FA-const). The fully actuated platforms generate the complete commanded force instantaneously, whereas UA generates its projection onto the current body-fixed thrust direction. The simulation uses a 12 s horizon, mass 1 .8 kg, gravitational acceleration 9 .81 m s −2, and a 35 N force limit. The horizontal velocity increases smoothly from zero to 0.6 m s −1 over the first 2 s, remains constant until 10 s, and then decreases symmetrically to zero. A −4 N horizontal external force acts from 4 s to 7 s, with 0 .2 s half-cosine transitions. The commanded force combines exact disturbance feed-forward with proportional–derivative position feedback having gain matrices diag (3, 10 ) and diag (4, 6). For UA and FA-opt, the desired pitch aligns the preferred body-force direction with the commanded force and is limited to 45 deg. The common pitch dynamics use gains 35 and 12, with angular-velocity and angular-acceleration limits of 55 deg s −1 and 180 deg s −2. The UA cost is the squared thrust magnitude. ## 9 Discussion and Conclusions 

This work distinguishes complete wrench capability from its minimum-cost use. For a fixed actuation geometry, zero-moment constraint, and actuator cost, the least costly body-force directions are determined jointly. Positive homogeneity makes these directions independent of force magnitude. Hence, for any prescribed position trajectory, aligning a fixed minimum-allocation direction with the required world-frame force confines the body force to a one-dimensional subspace and eliminates its transverse component. Underactuated-like force generation thus emerges from optimality rather than from limited force authority. The exact optimality-gap decomposition extends this result to full-pose trajectories. Its minimum class contains precisely the trajectories whose body-frame required-force direction remains in U★ almost everywhere, while the accumulated gap quantifies the additional allocation cost of every other attitude trajectory. For summed nonnegative actuator output, parallel thrust attains the universal force-generation lower bound whenever alignment and zero moment are feasible. Full actuation retains a complementary role during changes in the required-force direction. A fully actu-ated platform can immediately use transverse force and subsequently recover minimum-cost alignment, whereas a structurally underactuated platform must first reorient. Nominal efficiency can therefore favor underactuated-like motion while complete wrench capability supports tracking resilience. The results concern instantaneous force allocation under a zero body-moment constraint. Including the moment and actuator dynamics required for attitude motion couples allocation cost to attitude derivatives and requires trajectory-level optimization. Complete rigid-body dynamics, finite rotational inertia, and nonhomogeneous physical-power models are left for future work.
