# Vector Field Following for Quadrotors via Reduced Attitude Representations with Applications to Safety-Critical Control 

Mohammad Mirtaba and Max H. Cohen 

Abstract — Vector field tracking for quadrotors provides a systematic framework for specifying complex behaviors at the translational level and transferring them to the full vehicle dy-namics. Objectives such as safety, for example, can be encoded into a low-dimensional vector field, which is then embedded into the full dynamics through appropriate tracking conditions. The development of such tracking controllers for quadrotors can be facilitated by the fact that their translational dynamics depend only on the thrust direction and are independent of the vehicle’s heading. Thus, reduced-attitude representations, leveraging the geometry of the unit sphere, provide a natural framework for vector field tracking on quadrotors. In this work, we propose two vector field tracking controllers for quadrotors using reduced-attitude representations based on dynamic extension and geometric control, respectively. Next, we illustrate how safety can be encoded into the desired vector field using control barrier functions and enforced for the full system. The effectiveness of the proposed approaches is demonstrated via simulations and experimental flight tests. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Although commercially available quadrotors and flight controllers provide satisfactory flight stability and maneu-verability, ensuring safety during operation remains a sig-nificant challenge. Quadrotors are increasingly deployed in urban environments, often in cooperation with other robotic systems. Thus, imposing safety constraints is essential to pre-vent hazardous flight operations that could endanger nearby individuals, other robots, or surrounding infrastructure. Controllers leveraging attitude dynamics on SO(3) [1] require regulation of the entire orientation, including the heading direction (the rotation about the thrust vector), even though the heading does not directly influence the translational dynamics. Here, heading control authority is generated through differential rotor reaction torques, which are weaker than the moments used for tilting maneuvers [2]. Aggressive heading tracking may impose additional de-mands on actuators that could lead to saturation and degra-dation of translational stability. Since the thrust direction is the primary variable governing the translational dynamics, reduced-attitude approaches, leveraging the geometry of the unit sphere, have been developed to stabilize the thrust direction while leaving the heading unconstrained [3]. Although recent reduced-attitude control approaches de-couple translational dynamics from heading control, most existing designs rely on time-scale separation arguments, requiring significantly faster attitude tracking. This limita-tion, commonly encountered in cascaded controllers, has also been observed in full attitude control formulations on SO(3) ,

which has motivated recent research that explicitly considers the coupling between translational and rotational dynamics. Here, stability of the resulting cascade is often analyzed by treating the attitude tracking error as an input to the transla-tional subsystem, and requiring the translational dynamics to satisfy input-to-state stability (ISS) [4] or integral ISS (iISS) [5] properties. Although these properties are powerful for establishing stability of interconnected systems, they mainly offer qualitative guarantees in the sense that obtaining quanti-tative performance measures, such as convergence rates, can be challenging [6]. Moreover, enforcing such conditions may limit the selection of more aggressive position control laws and potentially affect transient performance. The literature on quadrotor control is now quite mature, but much research has focused on stabilization tasks. As noted earlier, however, recent advancements necessitate the consideration of safety specifications. Safety-critical control designs for quadrotors are facilitated by techniques such as reachability analysis [7] and model predictive control [8], which typically incur higher computational costs, and control barrier functions (CBFs) [9]. Examples of these aforemen-tioned approaches include trajectory optimization enforce-ment methods [10] that require high computational cost and neglect low level dynamics, model predictive path integral based method [11], CBFs designed for planar quadrotors [12], leveraging cascaded architectures for safety [13], and exploiting the equivalence between integrator chains and quadrotor dynamics via differential flatness [14]. Vector field tracking in robotic systems provides a means of specifying desired behaviors at a higher level and transfer-ring these behaviors to systems with complex dynamics [15]. This perspective is particularly relevant to safety-critical con-trol, where safety-preserving behaviors can be synthesized on simplified models and subsequently realized by the full-order arXiv:2610.06629v1 [eess.SY] 5 Oct 2026 system through rigorous tracking guarantees. For example, [16] synthesize CBFs on reduced-order models and then lift the resulting safety guarantees to the full nonlinear system by certifying convergence of the nonlinear dynamics to the reduced-order model (i.e., the vector field). In this paper, we leverage reduced-attitude representations to design quadrotor controllers that enforce exponential con-vergence of the translational dynamics to a desired vector field that carries safety properties. In particular, we 1) present two designs based on dynamic extension (DE) and geomet-ric control, respectively; 2) explicitly address translational– rotational coupling terms that arise in existing formulations, which present roadblocks to rigorous stability proofs; 3) ex-ploit the resulting exponential tracking guarantees to incorpo-rate safety constraints into the quadrotor control architecture. D. Problem Formulation 

The main objectives of this paper are twofold. First, given a smooth time-varying vector field vd : R3 × R → R3

that encodes a desired behavior for a quadrotor’s center of mass ˙p = vd(p, t ), design a feedback controller u = k(x, t )

such that along closed-loop trajectories, v(t) exponentially converges to vd(p(t), t ). Second, given a safety constraint on the quadrotor’s position, derive conditions on the vector field vd and feedback controller k such that completion of Objective 1 leads to safety of the closed-loop system. III. V ECTOR FIELD TRACKING 

In this section, we present two approaches to driving the quadrotor’s center-of-mass velocity v exponentially to a desired vector field vd(p, t ). Our first approach leverages dynamic extension, and the second approach employs geo-metric properties of the reduced-attitude representation.

VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we proposed two vector field tracking con-trollers for quadrotors based on reduced-attitude representa-tions. We established stability of the proposed dynamic ex-tension and geometric controllers, followed by a framework for encoding safety into the desired vector fields. Numerical simulations and indoor flight experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed approaches.
